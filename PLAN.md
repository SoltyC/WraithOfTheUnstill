## Q — The quality foundation (shared tech, before any new content)

Built once, used by the frost close-out *and* the meadow. All of it runs at load or at build
time; nothing creates a pipeline in play.

### Q1 — PBR materials: scans first, procedural layers on top

- `src/materials/` + `shaders/materials/*.wgsl.js`: a material is a **recipe** in
  `data/materials/*.json` — a scanned base (or a procedural one where no scan fits), plus
  procedural layers and **variants** (seed, hue/value/roughness ranges, layer weights), so bark,
  stone or moss is a family, not one texture.
- Procedural-only sets (glyph stone, Shaper crystal) are generated by compute shaders at load into
  the same texture arrays.
- Families needed across the game: Shaper ashlar (worn, chipped, frost-cracked), carved glyph
  stone, granite/schist rock, scree, bark ×4 (pine plates, birch, oak furrows, rowan), moss,
  lichen, soil, leaf litter, forest floor, felt, wool, timber, rope, bronze, ice.
- **Anti-tiling everywhere:** stochastic (hex-tile) sampling, macro variation and triplanar where
  surfaces are not UV'd. No visible repeat at any distance.
- **Weathering layers** in the shader, not baked: moss/snow by up-facing normal × cavity × a
  wetness/shade mask; rime in crevices; dirt at the ground line.
- **Hybrid, scans first (user, 2026-10-09: "lets use scanned use a hybrid, however scanned looks
  sick"):** CC0 photo-scanned PBR sets (ambientCG, Poly Haven; vendored, logged in ASSETS.md) are
  the base surface wherever a scan exists — bark, moss, forest floor, soil, leaf litter, rock,
  stone masonry, plaster, timber, felt. Procedural layers sit on top for variety and placement:
  macro colour/value variation, stochastic tiling of the scan, weathering (moss, snow, rime,
  dirt by normal × cavity × shade), cracks and breakage on generated geometry, and blends between
  scans. Pure procedural only where no scan fits (glyph carving, Shaper crystal, magic glow).
- Scans are shipped as compressed GPU textures (BC7 via `texture-compression-bc`, KTX2 container,
  transcoder vendored), 2K for hero materials and 1K otherwise, in texture arrays; a build step
  in `tools/textures/` converts and packs them. Memory budget tracked in PERF.md.
- Leaf and flower atlases: scanned leaves (CC0) where available, generated otherwise.

### Q2 — Procedural geometry generators (no repeats)

Seeded generators, run in workers at load (or `tools/` at build time and cached), producing
unique meshes per instance or large variant pools with per-instance shader variation.

- **Ruins and masonry** (`world/masonry.js`): replaces the box builders. Walls and columns are
  laid as *individual blocks* with bevelled, chipped edges, irregular courses, missing and fallen
  blocks, Voronoi-fractured breaks on ruined tops, drums with entasis, carved capitals. The
  existing site layouts in `world/architecture.js` stay; only their geometry is rebuilt. LODs:
  per-block at < 40 m, merged and simplified beyond.
- **Rocks** (`render/rocks.js`): domain-warped, eroded SDF → marching-cubes meshes, a pool of
  ~64 unique rocks per biome (boulders, outcrops, slabs, scree) + per-instance scale/rotation/
  skew; snow, moss and lichen as weathering layers.
- **Trees** (`tools/trees/` or worker): space colonisation for broadleaf, a whorl/apical model for
  conifers; per species a parameter space (height, crown shape, lean, damage, dead limbs,
  age). A pool of **~32 variants per species × 4–5 species**, plus per-instance colour, scale,
  lean and wind phase — so no two read alike. Leaves as clustered cards with procedurally
  generated leaf atlases (vein patterns, edge shapes, colour variation) — never single quads
  facing the camera.
- **Understory:** ferns (fronds on a parametric curve), broadleaf shrubs, saplings, grass clumps
  of several species, flowers (3–4 colours, shape families), fallen logs, stumps, branches.

### Q3 — Ecology placement

- `world/ecology.js`: per-cell rules from baked layers (height, slope, aspect, moisture from
  hydrology, sun exposure, biome weight) + simulated competition (Poisson-disc by species
  radius, canopy shade suppressing grass, saplings at clearings and log edges). Deterministic,
  CPU in a worker for colliders and placement, mirrored on the GPU for dense layers (grass,
  flowers).
- Clearings, forest edges, ruins drawing moss and saplings around them, paths worn through.

### Q4 — Foliage and vegetation renderer

- GPU-driven instancing: compute culling (frustum + Hi-Z occlusion — forests need it) into
  indirect draws; LOD chain per variant; **octahedral impostors** baked at load per variant,
  dithered cross-fade; nothing pops.
- Foliage shading: two-sided, translucency (thickness from the generator), specular sheen,
  alpha-to-coverage stabilised by TAA, wind from the M1 field (trunk sway, branch, leaf flutter).
- Shadows: foliage in the cascades with alpha test, far cascades from impostors; cached static
  cascades re-rendered only when the sun moves.

### Q5 — Lighting and atmosphere upgrades

- **Canopy light:** a baked canopy-density map → sky occlusion and green-tinted bounce under
  trees; dappled sun comes from the real shadow maps.
- **Volumetric shafts in the near field** (the post chain has shafts through the cascades at ¼
  res — extend with a froxel/near-volume for forest scale), dust motes and fireflies (GPU
  particles) living in them.
- **Local lights:** a small clustered point-light list (fires, lanterns, braziers, spell light)
  shared by terrain, architecture, characters and foliage — replaces the 4 spell-light slots.
- **Characters:** fix the robes reading black (albedo, sheen and SH ambient on cloth; rim light
  from sky); same for the Veiled.
- **Grade:** remove the grey veil — audit haze and exposure per time of day against the
  reference's contrast.

### Q6 — Performance strategy (to afford all this)

- The target is an **RTX 3060** (user, confirmed 2026-10-09; the BRIEF's 5070 Ti is superseded,
  DECISIONS.md "Target machine change"). The reference image's density at 1440p/90 fps on it needs:
  - **Render-scale + temporal upscale** (TAA already exists → TAAU at 67–77 % scale, then CAS),
    recorded in DECISIONS.md;
  - Hi-Z occlusion culling, impostors from ~120 m, foliage shadow LOD;
  - budgets re-split: vegetation 2.5 ms, terrain 1.5, shadows 1.8, post 2.0 (incl. upscale).
- A `?bench=forest` early (after Q4), before any content is built on top.

## Phase 8 — Highland Meadow and Gale

BRIEF §17: vegetation tech (trees, impostors, GPU grass with Flatten), the global wind field,
Gale verbs, glide, meadow Shaped and Warden, the hub settlement, rivers, rain and thunderstorm,
and the Frost↔Meadow border. **Gate:** the meadow's signature frame (a gust sweeping a silver-
green wave across a hillside with the Wraith's Gale lane cut through it); grass holds up in motion
from ground level to glide height.

The phase is large. It is split into milestones that each end in something reviewable; the risky
tech (grass, trees) comes first so a failure there changes the plan early, not late.

### M0 — Make the engine multi-biome (refactor, no new look)

Everything built so far assumes frost. Unpick that first, behind the same visuals.

- **Surface shading framework** (BRIEF §5.1): split the clipmap fragment into per-biome material
  modules (`shaders/surfaces/frost.wgsl.js`, `meadow.wgsl.js`, later `sand`, `mud`, `ash`) behind
  one interface — `surface(wp, N, state, fp) → {albedo, N, rough, sss, ...}` and a shared light
  function — blended by biome weight, evaluating only the materials whose weight is > 0 (uniform
  branch on the vertex-interpolated weights). Keeps `clipmap.wgsl.js` under the 800-line limit.
  Same pipeline count (one material, branches, not permutations).
- **Elements:** `game/bending/elements.js` — a registry `{ id, verbs: {tap, hold, heavy}, traversal }`;
  `systems/bending.js` dispatches to the active element instead of calling `frost` directly.
  Element switch on `1`–`6` / wheel, cuffs and hand-light colour by element (BRIEF §12). Frost is
  the only unlocked element until the meadow Warden is released.
- **Restoration per biome:** `game/worldState.js` holds six restoration values; environment,
  grade LUT, weather table and dialogue read the one for the biome under the player (blended
  across borders by biome weight).
- **Weather per biome:** `world/weather.js` gets a table per biome (frost: clear/overcast/snowfall/
  blizzard; meadow: clear/overcast/rain/thunderstorm), weighted at the border.
- **Save v3:** per-biome restoration, unlocked elements, active element, robe, the meadow chapter.
  v2 → v3 migration + round-trip test (`tests/save*.test.js`).
- **Quests per chapter:** `data/quests/meadow.json` loaded beside `frost.json`; one graph, quest ids
  namespaced. Test that both chapters load and their flags don't collide.

Acceptance: every existing test passes, frost shots are pixel-identical to before (capture diff),
0 late pipelines.

### M1 — Global wind field

- `world/wind.js` + `shaders/wind.wgsl.js`: a 2D wind texture (e.g. 256² over 1 km, scrolled
  with the player) = baked climatology (`wind.rg8`) × weather × restoration + **travelling gust
  fronts** (wide bands moving downwind at 6–12 m/s with a smooth profile) + small turbulence. One
  compute pass per frame; sampled by grass, trees, cloth, spindrift, snowfall, sound and glide.
- Gale verbs inject into it (a Gust is a short, strong local front).
- Replaces the ad-hoc `windStrength × weather.wind` in `systems/environment.js`.
- Acceptance: cloth and spindrift unchanged in frost; a debug view of the field in the overlay.

### M2 — Meadow ground (terrain material)

- `meadow.wgsl.js`: soil, short turf, moss, leaf litter under trees, exposed rock and scree, from
  the Q1 material sets (stochastic tiling, weathering layers), with the snow's three-scale detail
  rule (BRIEF §18: macro / ripples / grain). Forest floor driven by the Q3 canopy map.
- **Terrain state on grass:** footprints and verbs write `flatten` (word 2) and depression; mud
  shows where flattened ground is wet; healing rate per material (grass springs back in minutes,
  soil scars last).
- **Frost↔Meadow border as climate:** snow holds in hollows and on north faces first, thins to
  patches over turf, then a frost-burnt grass band (BRIEF §18: "transitions read as geology and
  climate, not as a blend mask"). Driven by height, aspect and the biome weight — not the
  weight alone.
- Photo spots: `p8-border-*`, `p8-meadow-ground-*` (no vegetation yet; judge the ground alone, as
  Phase 2 did for snow).

### M3 — GPU grass (the heaviest item; budget 1.5 ms with trees)

- **Generation:** a compute pass fills blade instances per clipmap ring around the camera
  (jittered grid, density from the material map, biome weight and slope; species/height/colour
  from noise), frustum- and distance-culled on the GPU into an indirect draw. No CPU per blade.
- **Blades:** curved strips (3–5 segments) near, thinning with distance to wider clumps, then to a
  grass-tinted terrain term far away (the ground shader darkens/greens where grass stands, so the
  far field matches the near field's colour and the transition is invisible).
- **Wind:** bend from the M1 field per blade (gust fronts read as rolling silver-green waves —
  the signature frame: the blade's sheen flips as it bends away from the sun).
- **Flatten:** blades read `flatten` and depression from the terrain state; footprints, the surf
  groove, Gust lanes and Shaped tracks lay grass down; it rises again as the state heals.
- **Shading:** translucency toward the sun, specular sheen along the blade, AO toward the root,
  shadow receive from the CSM; contact with the ground shader's colour so they don't float.
- **Flowers and tall stems** as a second species set in the same pass.
- Risk check: measure on T after M3 before continuing. If over budget: fewer near blades with
  wider shapes, half-rate wind for far rings, a lower cut-off distance with a better far term.

### M4 — Trees, understory and impostors

Built on Q2–Q4 (generators, ecology, renderer); this milestone is the content: 4–5 species
(highland pine, birch, oak, rowan, a dead/broken form), ~32 variants each with per-instance
variation; ferns, shrubs, saplings, flowers, fallen logs and stumps; groves, edges and clearings
placed by Q3; trunks collide (`solids.js` circles). Judged against
`screenshots/reference/forest-ruins-target.webp` at the same framing.

### M4b — God rays through the canopy (user, 2026-10-09: "light filtering through the leaves")

- The post chain already marches the sun through the shadow cascades for light shafts, and the
  trees cast into them; clear air has almost no haze, so the shafts never show. Add a **forest
  haze**: a thin in-scattering medium that thickens under canopy (a coarse canopy-density field
  from the tree placement, uploaded when it changes) and toward dawn/dusk, so sunlight broken by
  the crowns reads as beams.
- Shaft march: extend its reach and resolution under trees (the near-field volume), keep it
  temporal (TAA integrates the jitter), let the beams carry the key light's warm colour.
- Motes and pollen drifting in the beams (GPU particles like the diamond dust, lit only where the
  sun reaches them).
- Dappled light on the floor comes from the same tree shadows (already there).
- **Done 2026-10-10:** the forest haze, the shaft ratio for it, the canopy measure and the shade
  lift; photo spot `p8-forest-shafts`. **Open:** motes and pollen in the beams, a dawn/dusk
  thickening, and a sky-occlusion term under the crowns (the near floor is still very dark).

### M5 — Rivers and water

- **Water surface** from the baked hydrology (`hydro.rgba8`): a river mesh/ribbon along channels
  with a flow map, depth absorption tint, foam at rocks and banks, refraction of the bed, SSR
  (existing post pass). Lakes reuse the same shader still.
- **Frost bridges** (BRIEF §2.4, Metroidvania): Crystallize on river water freezes a walkable
  span (terrain-state `frozen` + a permanent ice transform); persists across saves.
- Rivers rise at springs in the meadow; the frozen spring of the frost chapter thaws on frost
  release and feeds one — a visible link between chapters.

### M6 — Gale verbs

All read and write the terrain state, ease in and settle out (BRIEF §9.1), and emit light.

- **Gust (tap):** a lance down the aim that cuts a flattened lane (brush FLATTEN + light strip)
  through grass, snow and ash, shoves Shaped and loose snow/debris; injects a gust front into the
  wind field so grass beyond the lane ripples.
- **Vortex (hold):** a swirling column at the aim that strips surface material in a ring (new
  brush STRIP: depression ring, mass thrown to the rim), holds debris aloft (GPU particles,
  pooled), drags light Shaped in; on release the debris settles back as mass.
- **Updraft (heavy):** a rising column; lifts the Wraith (glide entry), Shaped (staggered, then
  dropped) and debris. Leaves a blown-out ring.
- **Cross-element reactions** (BRIEF §4.4): Gust over wet slush throws spray and freezes it to
  rime; Vortex on snow builds a drift wall; Updraft through a Ribbon carries the water up as
  falling droplets. A table in `game/combat/reactions.js` (tested, BRIEF §19).
- Tests: every verb writes the state; reaction table; zero allocation (`npm run alloc`).

### M7 — Glide

- A traversal mode in the controller beside surf (`character/glide.js`): launched from a ridge,
  a jump at speed, or an Updraft; lift from the wind field (rising air over windward slopes, gust

  carries speed into a surf or a run.
- **Robe as wings:** a glide cloth pose (sleeves and mantle spread, hem streaming), roaring cloth

### M8 — Meadow Shaped (three archetypes + an elite)

The meadow's Shaped were the Shapers' **field-hands**: made from grass, turf, reed and the
standing stones, they sowed on the wind, kept the boundaries and called the breezes over the
uplands. Their work needed moving air. Stilled, each is stuck in its old task with nothing to do
it with — and that, not malice, is what makes them dangerous. They are made of the meadow, they
rise out of it, and they fall back into it (a windrow of cut grass, a sod that settles; the grass
grows back as the state heals). Their light is a warm seed-amber, not the frost's cold blue.

**Swarmer — the Sedgerunners (sowers)**
- *Body:* lean, long-legged runners of plaited sedge and seed-heads, a ruff of awns at the neck,
  a seed-pod core glowing amber. Light — a Gust can bowl one over.
- *What they were:* they ran the fields in loose flocks throwing seed into the wind.
- *Stilled:* with no wind to sow into they stand frozen in the tall grass, heads up, like hares
  — invisible until the grass parts. Close enough, the flock **flushes**: they break in all
  directions and come back in sweeping passes, slashing as they cross (never a straight rush).
- *Reading them:* the grass tells you — lines of parting stems, seed puffs where one crouches.
  They lay grass flat where they run (terrain-state `flatten`).
- *Reactions:* Gust scatters and topples them; Sweep soaks them heavy and slow; Crystallize on
  wet sedge makes them brittle (a frozen one shatters into chaff).
- *Nuance:* they herd — passes push the Wraith toward a Hedgewarden's boundary or a Chanter's
  pollen. In the restored meadow, a flock that is left alone sows on the wind and drifts away.

**Bruiser — the Hedgewarden (boundary-keeper)**
- *Body:* a hunched mound of turf sods over a ribcage of drystone and old roots; a lichened
  standing stone carried on its back like a burden; root-hands. Slow, heavy, deliberate.
- *What it was:* the Shapers grew the hedges and laid the walls with them.
- *Stilled:* still walking its boundary, still mending walls that no one breaks — **it ignores
  the Wraith until it crosses its line** (a wall, a hedge, a ring of stones; the boundary is
  visible). Then it is relentless inside its bounds and gives up at the edge.
- *Attacks:* tears up a strip of turf and rolls it at you as a travelling wave (terrain-state
  mound); slams the stone down to raise a ridge of earth; when hurt it **roots** — sinks its
  hands, grows a hedge-wall in front of itself (real cover, for both sides) and mends its turf.
- *Weak point:* the heartwood under the sods. Vortex strips the turf away; Ribbon soaks the
  roots; Crystallize freezes them brittle; then a heavy blow breaks it.
- *Nuance:* it can be avoided entirely. Some quests are about crossing its land, not killing it.
  Restored, the boundary-keepers go back to laying walls and stop guarding them.

**Caster — the Chanter (wind-caller)**
- *Body:* tall, stilt-legged, heron-like, of bundled reed; a hollow bell-flower head; seed-pods
  along its neck that pulse when it sings. Stands motionless in shallows and tall grass.
- *What it was:* it sang breezes across the fields.
- *Stilled:* its song makes stillness instead. It breathes out **pollen that hangs in the dead
  air** (BRIEF: stilled pollen) — curtains that blind (a soft, gold haze cutting sight) and slow;
  inside them focus does not return. It keeps its distance and wades away.
- *Support:* its song roots wounded Shaped — they slowly regrow turf while it sings (kill or
  silence it first). A hum before the song lets you hear where it stands.
- *Reactions:* Gust disperses the curtain and staggers it mid-song; Crystallize freezes the
  hanging pollen into glittering dust that falls; Ribbon washes it down to mud; Updraft carries
  the whole curtain away.
- *Nuance:* after the meadow Warden's release, the Chanters sing the wind for real — they no
  longer attack, and become part of the restored land (ambient, a rare sight at dusk).

**Elite — the Old Hedge**
- A Hedgewarden grown for an age round a Shaper ruin until it is half tree: a canopy of thorn
  and briar, the ruin's columns in its ribs. A side-quest fight in a grove; its boundary is the
  grove itself. Briar lashes, a hedge-maze grown round the Wraith, Sedgerunners nesting in it.

**Shared build**
- Chunk rigs and the Shaped shader gain material variants (plaited sedge, turf sod with soil and
  hanging roots, reed, drystone, seed-pod glow) — scanned grass/soil/stone layers, one pipeline,
  variant by uniform. Gait and AI reuse `game/shaped/`; new behaviours: hide-and-flush (runners),
  boundary territory (hedgewarden), keep-away + support (chanter).
- Each has its sound: a dry rustle and seed rattle; a stone grind and root creak; a hollow reed
  hum.
- Tests: boundary logic, flush trigger, pollen focus rule, restored-state behaviour.

### M9 — The Meadow Warden

Proposal (user to choose or replace): **the Bellwether** — a ~40 m long-necked grazer of turf
and Shaper standing stones whose ribs are a great stone bellows; it holds the wind in by breathing
in and never out. Its arena is a ring of standing stones on a hill.

- **Fight structure** (reuse the hybrid framework: `game/warden/fight.js` generalised into phases
  per Warden):
  1. *Stilled air:* it inhales the wind; grass lies rigid; its stone vents are sealed with turf —
     Gust/Vortex strips the turf from each vent.
  2. *Climb:* root-bound holds on its flanks and neck (the existing hold-to-hold climb; BRIEF
     §8.4); open the bellows' valves from on the body while it rears and shakes.
  3. *Its breath:* every N s it exhales a gale that sweeps the arena — shelter behind standing
     stones (the barrage pattern, now a wind wall that flattens grass and shoves the Wraith).
  4. Frenzy: it calls the meadow Shaped (cap 8).
- **Release:** it exhales for the first time in an age; a wind front rolls out over the whole
  meadow and the grass moves — the gate's signature moment. Restored meadow: grass waves, pollen
  drifts, rivers run faster, birdsong in audio.
- Grants Gale and glide.
