# Dialogue style guide (frost pass, for the meadow chapter)

Tone (BRIEF 2.2, 11): mythic and melancholic, but spoken. Few words, and each one means something.

## Rules
1. Clear first. The player must know who is speaking, what happened, what is asked, and where to go.
2. Introduce yourself by name the first time an NPC speaks ("I am Aud, keeper here").
3. Name people and places plainly. Say "Tobin" not "the boy", on first mention at least. Use the exact map name ("the Pilgrims' Fire", "the Watching Stone").
4. Objectives are short imperatives that start with a verb and a place: "Go to ...", "Talk to ...", "Find ...", "Kill ...", "Ring ...".
5. Natural modern English. No inverted syntax ("Nine winters it hung there"), no riddles, no private metaphors.
6. One image per line, at most. Short sentences. If a line hints, add the plain fact beside it.
7. NPCs sound like people under strain, not like poets. Plain verbs, concrete nouns.
8. Keep ids, flags and numbers untouched. Keep any phrase a test matches (e.g. "ridge breathes").

## Good / bad
1. Bad: "You stood up out of his robe." Good: "You woke inside the Shaper's robe. I am Aud, keeper of this monastery."
2. Bad: "I was following the quiet." Good: "I'm not lost. I was listening."
3. Bad: "Isolde has her own errand, for the dark hours." Good: "Isolde needs you after dark."
4. Bad (objective): "A keeper named Aud was waiting." Good: "Talk to Aud at the monastery."
5. Bad: "The shrine, then the Run. The slope remembers how they rode it." Good: "Rest at the shrine, then take the Run. Lean forward and the slope will carry you."
6. Bad: "Learn on that one." Good: "Practice on it. Climb it, break it, and when it stills, put my stone between you and it."
