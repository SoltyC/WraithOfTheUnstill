# Storyline: what the stillness holds (proposal, 2026-11-22)

**User direction (2026-11-22):** *"Create a storyline, and the storyline can be influenced by players' decisions. Players have to be forced to make extremely hard ethics decisions or moral decisions… and their decisions, not just ethics or moral decisions, should influence how the story actually goes."*

This sits on top of `docs/direction.md` (the loop, the hybrid guidance, people who exist) and grows out of what's already built and written:
- the Ebbhold twist (`docs/phase-10/storylines.md`): the Drowned were alive, held;
- the existing characters: Aud, Maren, Varo, Tobin, Wenna, Hild, Marit, Ebb, Doran, Corin, Sarai, Iven, Nadra, Pell, Morrow, Liese.

Names, places and Warden fights stay as they are unless marked.

---

## 1. The spine

### 1.1 The truth, which the player learns in pieces

Forty years ago the world's motion ran wild. The Shapers had driven it too hard, and it broke loose:
- a firestorm on the meadow's wind;
- a flood through Ebbhold's sea-doors;
- the erg marching on the caravan cities;
- the volcano opening.

The last Shapers couldn't stop the disasters, so they **stopped the moment**. Each Warden was set to hold its land still *in the middle of its disaster*, with everyone in it alive between one breath and the next. They meant it to last a season, until someone could fix what was broken. Nobody came. The stillness that saved those people is now slowly killing everyone else: the wind is dead, the crops fail, the rivers stand.

The Veiled have forgotten all of this. They see the Wraith as salvation, and every release looks like healing.

**So every Warden is a question, not an enemy:** *what is this one holding, and who is inside it?*

### 1.2 The arc of what the player knows

| Stage | What the player believes | Where it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust** | releasing heals the land | the frost: a release that really is clean (it held only the snow, so the last Shaper could make the Wraith) |
| **Doubt** | some people don't want the releases, and one of them may be right | the meadow: Doran |
| **Knowledge** | every Warden holds people alive | Ebbhold, or earlier if the player found Tobin (§3) |
| **Responsibility** | every release from now on is a choice about who lives | the Ember Waste, and the coast |

Open order after the frost means the meadow, the dunes and the fen can come in any order. **That order changes the story.** Doing the dunes after Ebbhold makes the dunes' choice a different, harder choice, because the player knows what "held" means.

---

## 2. Rules for a hard choice

1. **No right answer.** Both sides have a person the player has met and likes. If one side is obviously correct, it's a test, not a dilemma.
2. **Chosen by an act, not a menu.** Where you steer the Walker, whom you carry, what you tell Hild, which doors you leave shut. This keeps BRIEF's "one choice deep" dialogue rule and keeps the player inside the world. The game doesn't pause, highlight the choice or say "this will be remembered".
3. **Made with partial knowledge.** The player knows enough to choose and not enough to be sure. The hybrid guidance (`direction.md` §2.2) decides how much they know: a player who listened to more people chooses with more light.
4. **No meter, no labels.** There is no good or evil bar. The only score is what *people* do: who trusts the Wraith, who follows it, who won't trade, who is alive and where.
5. **Delayed and visible consequences that travel.** A choice in the meadow shows up in the fen. People move between biomes because of what the player did.
6. **Never a game over.** Every branch reaches the end. "Failure" is a different world, not a reload.
7. **Choices that aren't moral count too.** The order of biomes, how long you take (people travel on their own clocks), who you helped first, whether you led Tobin home. The player often won't know these were choices until later.

## 3. Chapter by chapter

Each chapter has **one great choice** and a few small ones. The state each choice writes is listed in §4.

### Frost: trust (built; light additions)

- **The release is clean,** on purpose: the player learns to trust the pattern, so the doubt later lands.
- **Small choice, already in the data: Tobin.** *"Lead Tobin back to the fire, or leave him listening."*
  - **Led home:** he's safe, and Maren is grateful.
  - **Left listening:** Tobin has been hearing voices under the ice that nobody believes. He leaves the camp on his own after First Snow. He turns up in Ebbhold's undercroft, ahead of the Wraith, with his ear to the doors: *"They're talking. They're asking if it's over."*

  **A player who let a child keep listening learns the truth before the Listener's release** (the "learn before" branch, `storylines.md` alternative 1). Nothing marks this as a choice at the time.
- **Small choice: the den mother.** Clear the den as Maren asks, or drive the mother off and leave the young (the Wraith releases, it doesn't kill). If the den is spared, hounds hunt the snowline border later, and Maren is cool towards the Wraith. If it's cleared, Tobin (if he's home) won't speak to it again.

### Meadow: doubt — *The Gatekeeper*

**What the Bellwether holds:** the firestorm, held in the manor's chimneys, and the kiln workers of the manor, among them **Doran's daughter**, who climbed to the manor at sixteen to see the bell, the year the stillness began. She has been inside the hold ever since.

**The fire night was Doran's doing.** He knew the Wraith was coming down from the north. He set the hearths so the village would be busy, afraid, and kept off the hill: the stair is the one place he can keep the Wraith from. He didn't mean anyone to be hurt. Ebb nearly burned.

**The pieces (found, never told):**
- the lamp oil smell on the stair-foot gate;
- Ebb saw "the gate-man" at his house;
- Doran's hands are blistered;
- his barks the night after: *"It goes up the hill, it kills what's up there. You don't know what's up there."*
- in the manor (before the release, during the climb), a girl in a kiln-worker's apron stands still at a window, a lamp in her hand that hasn't flickered in forty years.

**The great choice: what the player does with the truth.**
- **Tell Hild** (bring Ebb to her, or the oil): justice for Marit and Ebb.
  - Doran is cast out.
  - He walks north into the frost. He's found again at a frost shrine, alive if Isolde's fire is lit, dead if not.
  - Hearn trusts the Wraith fully.
- **Keep his secret:** mercy for a father who was right.
  - Marit never knows who nearly killed her son.
  - Doran stays, a broken man, and at the release he climbs into the manor to be with his daughter, and is lost with her.
- **Tell Doran what you saw in the manor** (talk to him after climbing): he asks the Wraith to wait while he goes up first to say goodbye.
  - If the Wraith waits a night, he comes back down and leaves with Wenna, and the release takes only his daughter.
  - If it doesn't wait, he's in the manor when it goes.

**What it seeds:** the first hint that the held might be alive. Most players will put it down to a grieving father. That's the point.

**Not changed:** the Bellwether fight itself (standing rule). Doran's fate changes the scenes around it, and possibly one figure inside the manor during the climb, which needs the user's word.

### Dunes: the cost of a home — *Where the Walker lies down*

**What the Walker holds:** the march of the erg. When it walked, the dunes walked with it, burying cities. Stilled, it let people settle in places the sand used to cross.

**Two communities now live where they shouldn't be able to:**
- **Sarai's Wellhouse camp** (Pell, Nadra), at the dry well;
- **Iven's glass-readers,** in the Sun Gate ruin, which only stands above the sand because the erg stopped.

**The great choice: where the player steers the Walker home** (pure play, using the steering that's already designed; the Walker fight is not off limits).
- **The Wellhouse:**
  - the well fills and the camp thrives;
  - the erg starts to move again, *away* from the well, and within a season the sand buries the Sun Gate;
  - Iven's people walk west to Hearn, and some don't make it;
  - Iven, if he lives, will not speak to the Wraith.
- **The Sun Gate:**
  - it lies down there, and the gate's old cistern fills;
  - the Wellhouse well stays dry, and the camp has to leave the erg;
  - Pell and Nadra go to the fen, and the player meets them again at Morrow's jetty.
- **Neither** (the Wraith lets it walk on, never lying down): the Walker keeps its circuit, and the erg moves again everywhere. Both communities must leave, but both get a season's warning, and nobody dies. Sand-surf is granted, but the dunes are never "restored", and the erg is never calm.

**Order changes the meaning.** Before Ebbhold, it's a choice between two villages. After Ebbhold, the player knows to ask *what is the Walker holding inside it?* That opens a side thread for a returning player only: the caravanserai on its back holds a caravan of the held. Where it lies down decides whether they wake to water or to sand.

### Mire and Ebbhold: knowledge — *The Register* (designed; how it branches)

- **The base story is unchanged** (`storylines.md`): Morrow cuts the Lung, the names, the release, the veils on the water.
- **It branches on what the player brings in:**
  - **Tobin left listening (frost):** Tobin is in the undercroft and the player learns before the Listener's release. The great choice becomes open: release the Listener (the fen lives, the held drown), or **don't**, and leave the city held. Then the fen keeps dying, Morrow never forgives the Wraith, and Tide stays lent, never mastered. Ebbhold's critical path ends either way.
  - **Doran's story (meadow):** a player who saw his daughter at the window will recognise the Drowned's stillness. A small Remembrance line links the two; the player does the rest.
  - **The names (6, `direction.md` M3)** and **the shut doors** decide the survivors' count, as designed.
  - **Pell and Nadra** (if the Wellhouse was abandoned) are at the jetty. Pell is the first one to ask the Wraith, after the veils: *"Did you know?"*

### Ember Waste: responsibility — *The Mountain Is Waking* (full design: `docs/phase-11/ember-design.md`)

**The user's direction (2026-11-22):** the chapter is about evacuating villages and saving people.

- The Ember Warden's hold on the volcano is breaking.
- Someone the Wraith knows brings the warning, and who it is depends on earlier choices (Iven, Nadra, or a miner's boy).
- The Wraith climbs the mountain and reads the signs with its verbs: new steam, tilted survey stones, a killing hollow, a soured spring, and the lava lake rising in the crater.
- Four very different peoples have to be convinced, each by a different kind of proof and through deeds: miners, pilgrims, farmers and fishers.
- They depend on each other to get out.
- Trust is shown in their bodies, never as a meter, and the Wraith's reputation from earlier chapters arrives before it.
- Wrong choices (force, lies, the wrong proof, broken promises, wasted time) cost lives when the mountain erupts. The eruption is a playable climax of last rescues and the Warden fight.
- The quiet choice: the Wraith can give its own health threads to the failing hold to buy days.

### Coast: the last question (outline)

**What the sea Warden holds:** the last of the world's motion, and the Wraith itself. The Wraith is the last Shaper's held moment, and it was always going to end at the water (`storylines.md`: *"Do not let them send you to the water."*).

There is one ending: the Wraith gives itself to the sea (§5). The state decides what that leaves behind.

## 4. The state the story reads

A small set, saved in the quest graph's flags and vars (save v3). No meter is ever shown.

| State | Written by | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| **Fates:** per named person, alive or dead, and where they are | every great choice; travel on people's own clocks | placement, barks, who appears in later biomes, the ending's people |
| **Communities:** Wellhouse, Sun Gate, Hearn, frost camp, Ebbhold survivors, the ember town (where each is and how many) | the dunes' choice, Ebbhold's count, Ember's evacuation | the world map's settlements, trade, the coast's gathering |
| **Word:** what each community has heard about the Wraith (revered, uneasy, feared). It travels between settlements with the refugees and the pilgrims | how the player treated that community's people, and what refugees tell | trust (who follows, who trades, who listens to a warning) |
| **Knowledge:** what the Wraith has learned of the truth (Tobin, Doran's daughter, the frost library, the kiln-master, the Walker's caravan) | exploration | which ending is open; the "learn before" branches; Remembrance |
| **Held:** each Warden released, left held, or how it was released (where, after what preparation) | the great choices | the land's restoration, the coast |
| **Order and time:** which biome came when, and the days passed | play | the meaning of later choices; where travellers are |

## 5. The ending: the last tide (user, 2026-11-22)

**The user's direction:** *"At the end we want the player to make a choice. The only way they can complete the game is to sacrifice themselves after a cutscene, and thus it resets their progress, and then it goes to the credits."*

**There is one ending.** The only way to finish the game is for the Wraith to give itself to the sea. The player's choices decide what that sacrifice leaves behind, not whether it happens.

### 5.1 The sequence

1. **The shore.** With the other Wardens released, the sea is the last still thing. The coast's people gather on the shore, and who is there is the story state (§4):
   - the living, the moved and the missing;
   - Wenna if she lived;
   - Morrow on her jetty;
   - the ember survivors' camp;
   - Doran if he's alive.
2. **The last cutscene, *The Last Tide*.** The sea Warden rises, and the truth is told plainly:
   - the motion in the robe is the last of the world's motion;
   - the tide can turn only if the Wraith lets go of it.

   The last Shaper's journal line comes back if the player found it: *"Do not let them send you to the water."*
3. **The choice is an act, not a menu.** Control returns on the shore, facing the water.
   - **Walk into the sea:** the player has to keep walking (hold forward) as the water rises and the robe grows heavy, as with the Listener's release (the player's own hand does it).
   - **Turn back:** this is allowed at any point before the water closes over the cowl. The game doesn't end. The sea stays still, the shore waits, and the people on it say less each time the Wraith comes back. The player can go anywhere and return whenever they choose.
   - **Nothing else completes the game.** "Become the stillness" and "give the motion away" are no longer endings.
4. **The sacrifice.** Once the cowl light goes under:
   - **the save is erased** at that moment, so a reload can't undo it;
   - the sacrifice plays as an unskippable cutscene: the robe empties and sinks, the tide turns, and motion runs back up every river and across every released land.
5. **The epilogue.** This is the world the player made: a long, slow pass along the coast and inland, composed from the state. It shows:
   - who lives where, and which camps moved;
   - the veils or the survivors on the fen;
   - the ember villages, standing or ash;
   - Doran, Pell, Tobin and Iven, where they ended up;
   - the empty robe washed up on the sand, and who finds it.

   **Wenna's variant** (earned: the frost library's knowledge, Wenna alive, and at least two communities whose Word is not "feared"): she is the one who walks into the shallows and picks up the robe, and the motion that was in it goes into her. She is the first Shaper in forty years. The last shot is hers.
6. **The credits,** over the sea moving again.
7. **The title screen.** There is no save, so the only way in is a New Game. The world starts still again, waiting for someone.

### 5.2 Rules

- **The loss is the point.** Everything the player built (the weapons, the forge, the marks, the people saved) is gone from their save. What remains is what they saw in the epilogue. The game says nothing to soften it.
- **Never by accident.**
  - The walk takes long enough (about 20 s of holding forward through rising water) that no one does it by mistake.
  - Turning back is always possible until the cowl goes under.
  - Nothing on screen asks "Are you sure?": the walk itself is the confirmation.
- **Nothing is remembered** (user, 2026-11-22). After the sacrifice the game keeps no record at all: not the save, not a flag, not a changed title screen. A returning player starts exactly as a new one does.
- **The cutscene rule:** *The Last Tide* is skippable after the first viewing like any other cutscene, but the sacrifice and the epilogue are never skippable, like the Listener's release.

### 5.3 What it needs (build notes)

- **Save:** an erase that also clears the browser's backup copies, and a test that no save survives the sacrifice.
- **A credits screen:** the minimal dark UI style (memory: menus are dark glass, hairlines and a gold accent), over a live view of the sea.
- **The shore gathering:** placement from the state.
- **The walk into the sea:** the dive's water and robe already exist (U1, U2). The new parts are a slow forced walk, the cowl light going under, and the turn-back exit.
- **Composing the epilogue:** shot variants by state, using the cutscene tool in `docs/people-and-cutscenes.md` §3.4.
- **Bot tests:**
  - a run that turns back (the save survives, the game continues);
  - a run that walks in (the save is gone, credits roll, and the title offers only a New Game).

## 6. Scope and order

- **Content per great choice:** 2–3 outcomes. Each is mostly placement, barks, one scene variant and the state, not a separate chapter. The one ending's epilogue is composed from the state.
- **Build order (fits `direction.md`):**
  1. **The state model** (§4) and a debug page that shows it.
  2. **The meadow's Doran story** with the M1 rework. It replaces the fire night's "unknown cause", and the fire night's bug fixes come first anyway.
  3. **Tobin's branch** in the frost (data only, plus one figure in the undercroft).
  4. **The Walker's destinations** with M2.
  5. **Ebbhold's branches** with M3.
  6. **Ember and the coast:** written fully when their phases start.
- **Bot tests:** a script per branch that plays to its outcome and checks the state and placement. The quest template rule ("the bot can finish it") holds for every branch.

## 7. What this changes in the rules

- **BRIEF §2.2** ("the Wraith releases them"): the "how" and "when" of a release are choices everywhere; whether to release at all is open from Ebbhold on (decision 3).
- **The quest template's "at most one choice":** stays at one per conversation. The great choices are made by acts, so dialogue stays one choice deep.
- **The quest template's "no irreversible failure":** still holds. Choices are irreversible, but none of them is a failure.
- **The Bellwether and the frost Warden fights:** untouched. Anything near them (a figure in the manor, Doran inside at the release) is listed for the user's decision.

## 8. Decisions for the user

2. **Doran set the fire:** is it right for the meadow, given the fire night is being reworked anyway?
3. **Leaving a Warden held:** from Ebbhold on, or never? (The Ember Warden is no longer a candidate: its hold breaks whatever the player does; the player buys time instead.)
4. ~~The third ending~~ (answered 2026-11-22: one ending, the sacrifice, which erases the save and rolls the credits; Wenna's variant lives in its epilogue, §5).
5. **The meadow release:** may a scene change near the Bellwether (Doran in the manor), without touching the fight?
