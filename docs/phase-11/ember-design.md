# The Ember Waste: *The Mountain Is Waking* (design, 2026-11-22)

**User direction (2026-11-22):** *"For the volcano and the biome, it really should be more of the player trying to evacuate villages and save people… someone says that the volcano will erupt… you go up the mountain and look for signs… you look in and you can see lava boiling… but the villagers don't trust you, so you have to make the best decisions possible to get them to trust you… if you make the wrong decisions, the villagers won't trust you, the volcano will erupt, and they'll all die. We can have multiple villages, but they need to feel different and not just copy and paste the one in the meadow."*

This replaces the Ember outline in `docs/storyline.md` §3 and fits its rules (§2): choices are acts, there's no meter, and consequences are delayed and travel. Guidance follows `docs/direction.md` §2.2: the goal is shown and the way is found.

---

## 1. The chapter in one breath

The Ember Warden's hold on the volcano is breaking. Every release elsewhere has put motion back into the world, and the mountain is waking with it.

Someone the Wraith already knows brings the warning. The Wraith climbs the mountain and *reads* it: steam where there was none, survey stones tilted by a swelling cone, a hollow where the air itself kills, and at the summit a lava lake rising in the crater, with the Warden straining inside it.

Below, four communities live on the richest and most dangerous ground in the world. None of them has a reason to believe a hooded thing from the north, and some of them have heard what happened on the fen.

**The chapter is a race to be believed.** The eruption is coming either way. How many people are alive afterwards depends on what the player read, whom they told, what they showed them, and what they promised.

**Pillars:**
- **5, learn by reading:** the mountain tells the truth, and the people have to be read too.
- **2, the world remembers and answers:** trust is built by deeds in the world.
- **3, release, not kill:** the Warden is still released, and *when* is a choice.

**Loop layer:** the session (1–2 hours), and the campaign's state.

## 2. The warning: who brings it depends on the story so far

The messenger is chosen from the story state (`storyline.md` §4), so earlier choices decide who the player hears it from, and how believable that person is to the villages:

| If… | The messenger | What they bring | How the villages take them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iven lived (the Walker lay down at the Sun Gate) | **Iven**, the glass-reader | old glass from the cone's foot, fused in a pattern he has seen once before, in the glass of the war that made the erg | the farmers trade with the glass-readers and trust Iven |
| Pell and Nadra left the erg | **Nadra**, the mapmaker | her map of the cone, drawn twenty years ago, against the cone now: the north flank has grown | the miners respect a measurement |
| neither | **a miner's boy, Cole,** who ran down the mountain to Hearn after his father went into the tubes and didn't come up | his father's lamp, its glass crazed by heat | nobody listens to a child, so the player starts with no one vouched for |

- **The world warns too, from earlier in the game.** After the second release, there's a faint tremor felt in every biome, and ash falls on Hearn one evening. Varo watches the south and says *"Something's burning that hasn't burned in my life."* A player who went looking early finds the mountain already stirring.
- **Pacing.** The warning comes when the player first nears the Ember Waste, or when the third release happens, whichever is first. Until then the mountain is asleep, and the villages can be visited in peace. **A player who gets to know them early has an easier chapter.**

## 3. Reading the mountain (the evidence)

The climb is a route of **signs**, each a tableau the player reads with their verbs. No marker points at the signs. The messenger names the mountain, the villagers' own stories name places ("the hollow where the goats die", "the Shapers' stones on the north road"), and the landscape leads the eye.

| Sign | Where | What the player does | What it proves | Can be carried down as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **New steam** | fissures on the north flank, where the old maps show none | Frost on a fissure: the ice flashes to steam at once. Done again a day later, it flashes faster | the ground is heating, and fast | the player's word, or a villager brought to see |
| **The survey stones** | a line of Shaper levelling stones on the north road; their plumb-bobs hang outside the marks | Stone (Ridge) under one: the bob swings back true over level ground, so it's the mountain that moved, not the stone | the cone is swelling | the stone's bronze plumb-bob |
| **The still hollow** | a basin where goats lie dead with no wound, and birds on the ground | walking in, the cowl light gutters as it did with the Lung. Gale (Gust) clears it for a moment, and the light comes back | the mountain is breathing out what kills | a dead bird (the farmers and pilgrims read this) |
| **The sour spring** | the pilgrims' sacred spring, on the south flank | it has gone yellow and warm. Tide (Draw) lifts it, and the drawn water stinks of sulphur | the deep water is cooking | a flask of it |
| **The crater** | the summit, the set piece | the final climb through ash and heat. At the rim the player looks in and sees the lava lake churning, risen, and the **Ember Warden**, half sunk in it, its arms locked across the vent and its crust cracking | the hold is breaking. It isn't *if*, it's *days* | nothing can be carried. The only way to share it is to bring someone up |

**Evidence counts only where it's valued.** Each village believes a different kind of proof (§4). Showing the wrong proof to the wrong people does nothing, or worse.

**Bringing a person up the mountain** is the strongest proof there is, and the riskiest:
- an escort through heat, the still hollow and the Shaped;
- if the person is hurt, their village blames the Wraith;
- if they see the crater, their whole village moves.

## 4. Four villages, four different peoples

Each one differs from Hearn, and from the others, in:
- **where it sits and how it's built** (new kits, never Hearn's houses);
- **how it lives** (its daily sounds and work);
- **how it decides** (who has to be convinced);
- **what it believes as proof**;
- **what it fears about leaving**;
- **its hard choice.**

They **depend on each other** to get out, so the order the player works in matters.

### 4.1 Cinderhold: the miners

| | |
|---|---|
| **Place and build** | Cut *into* the lava tubes of the east flank: galleries in the rock, iron lifts and cages on chains, terraces of basalt, soot on everything, and lamps lit day and night. Vertical, not a village green. |
| **Life** | Hammers, the lift bells, carts on rails, and shifts changing at the gong. |
| **Who decides** | **The guild**: three masters who vote. The miners do what the guild says. |
| **What counts as proof** | **Measurement:** the survey stone's plumb-bob, Nadra's map, the heat of the deep seam (they keep a bell-thermometer). They laugh at omens. |
| **Their fear** | Losing the mine. They are in debt to the guild, and leaving means ruin. |
| **What they have that others need** | **The lower tube**, the only road under the western lava field to the coast, and the guild's lift keys. |
| **Their secret** | The tithe (`storylines.md`, *The Cage*): the guild has been lowering a cage of goods into the deep tube every month, to "something below", to keep the seams open. Lately the tithe is people: debtors. The guild masters know. The miners don't. |
| **The hard choice** | Expose the tithe, or keep it. Exposing it (taking a miner down to see the cage) turns the miners against the guild, and the miners believe the Wraith and leave. But the guild seals the lower tube, and the road to the coast closes for everyone. Keeping the secret means the guild deals: they will open the tube if the Wraith "forgets what it saw", and the debtors already below are left there. |

### 4.2 The Ash Chapel: the pilgrims

| | |
|---|---|
| **Place and build** | Cells cut in pumice on the *south* flank, closest to the crater: ash-plaster walls, prayer ribbons on poles, small bronze bells, and the sacred spring in a grotto. White and grey against black ground. |
| **Life** | Silence, bells at the hours, the spring tended, and pilgrims climbing with offerings. |
| **Who decides** | **The Seer, Annick**, an old woman everyone follows. |
| **What counts as proof** | **Omens in their own scripture:** the spring turning, the birds falling, the "mountain's breath". Measurements are an insult. |
| **Their fear** | Leaving the mountain is leaving their god. They believe the last Shaper's hearth is inside it, and that an eruption is a calling. |
| **What they have that others need** | **The pilgrims' path**, the only way round the north flank when the lava comes down the road. Only they know it in ash fall. |
| **The hard choice** | Annick will never leave. She reads the same omens the Wraith shows her, and decides it's the calling. The player can bring her faithful to doubt her, and most will leave without her. Or the Wraith can **take her by force** (Gale, a held Vortex): she lives, and never forgives. The pilgrims who saw it tell every village the Wraith carries people off, so trust falls everywhere it travels. Or the player can stay with her, at the end, and let the faithful go: she asks the Wraith to stand with her at the spring when it comes. |

### 4.3 The Sow: the farmers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Place and build** | Not a village: **scattered farmsteads** across the fertile ash plain to the west. Dry-stone walls, terraced vines and figs, cisterns, drying racks, goats and mules, low stone houses with vaulted roofs. The greenest place in the waste. |
| **Life** | Family by family: a harvest in, beasts in the fold, children running between farms. |
| **Who decides** | **Each family's elder.** There are six farmsteads and six decisions. Convincing one helps the next, because they talk over the walls. |
| **What counts as proof** | **Their own land:** the crack under their own barn, their cistern gone warm, their goats that won't go into the hollow. The player has to *point it out on their farm*. A sign from somewhere else is "the mountain's business". |
| **Their fear** | Losing the harvest and the beasts, which means starving next winter even if they live. "It always shakes in autumn." |
| **What they have that others need** | **Mules and carts**, for the old and the young of every village, on the long road. |
| **The hard choice** | The families will go only with their beasts and harvest, and that slows everyone on the road. The player can help them load, which costs time the other villages need. Or **tell them to leave it**: fewer will come, but those who do get out fast. One family's elder, Aled, will go only if the Wraith promises to come back for his flock. The player can promise or not. A broken promise travels too. |

### 4.4 Steamhaven: the fishers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Place and build** | **Stilt houses** on black sand where an old lava flow meets the sea. Steam where the waves hit warm rock, charred-timber boats, nets on frames, a long jetty. |
| **Life** | Boats out at dawn, the catch gutted on the jetty, songs. |
| **Who decides** | **The boat captains.** Each boat is a family, and each captain decides for their boat. |
| **What counts as proof** | **The sea:** they already know. The fish are gone from the warm bay, and the steam has crept along the shore. They don't need convincing the mountain is waking. |
| **Their fear** | **The Wraith itself.** If the word from the fen has reached them (veils on the water, the Drowned), they fear the Wraith more than the mountain. Their song for it is a warning to children. |
| **What they have that others need** | **The boats.** By sea is the only way out for the far side of the plain, and the fastest for everyone. |
| **The hard choice** | The boats hold so many. The captains will take their own, and then whoever the Wraith brings, **if they trust it**. When the last boats are filling, there's room for one more group, and two are on the jetty. |

### 4.5 How they lock together

```
             the pilgrims' path (north flank)
  Ash Chapel ─────────────────────────────┐
                                          ▼
  Cinderhold ── lower tube (under the lava field) ──► the coast road ──► Steamhaven boats
                                          ▲
  The Sow ── mules and carts (the old, the young) ┘
```

- Win Steamhaven's trust and the boats will wait, so the far villages have somewhere to go.
- Get the Sow moving and its carts carry the slow from every village.
- The tube (Cinderhold) and the path (the Chapel) are the two roads when the main road burns. Lose both, and only those who reached the boats early live.

**There is no right order,** only trade-offs the player reads from the people.

## 5. Trust: what wins it and what loses it

There's no bar and no number on screen. **The villages show it in their bodies**, and the player learns to read them (pillar 5):
- doors open or shut;
- children sent inside or left to watch;
- bundles packed by the door;
- carts loaded;
- people looking at the mountain;
- elders talking among themselves;
- someone coming out to meet the Wraith, or nobody.

Each village holds two things, and both are needed:
- **belief:** that the mountain will erupt;
- **trust:** that the Wraith is telling the truth and means them well.

**Wins it:**
- **the right proof, to the right people** (§4);
- **a deed for them, done unasked or asked:** shore up Cinderhold's cracked lift, put out a vine fire on the Sow, fish a drowned net free in the steaming bay, carry Annick's water up;
- **respect for their ways:** wait for the Chapel's bells to end before speaking; speak to the elder first on a farm; don't walk into a mine with a burning flail;
- **someone they trust vouching:** the messenger (§2), a refugee from the erg or Hearn who's settled here (story state), or a villager the Wraith took up the mountain;
- **keeping a promise.**

**Loses it:**
- **force and fear:** using an element near people, carrying someone off, a Shaped fight that wrecks a farm;
- **the wrong proof, pushed:** measurements at the Chapel, omens at the guild;
- **lies:** the player *can* say *"It goes tonight."* when it doesn't. It works fast, and the village runs. When the night passes, that village, and every one it talks to, stops believing the Wraith;
- **word from earlier chapters:** a Wraith feared after Ebbhold, or one that carried off a seer, starts behind. Refugees can carry its reputation in either direction;
- **broken promises;**
- **time:** the longer the Wraith is away, the more the mountain's signs feel normal. "It's been shaking for days and nothing came."

**Fairness rules** (pillar 5: no lying telegraphs):
- Early mistakes can be mended with deeds. Late ones can't, because there isn't time. That stakes are high late and forgiving early is honest pacing.
- Every village can be won in **at least two different ways.**
- The people's bodies always show the truth of where they stand. A player who watches can tell.

## 6. The clock

The eruption is driven by **stages**, not a countdown on screen. Each stage advances with in-game days *and* with the mountain's events. The player hears and sees each one everywhere in the Waste:

| Stage | What everyone sees | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Waking** | steam on the north flank, a tremor a day | villages calm; the time to make friends |
| **2. Swelling** | the survey stones tilt further, ash falls at night, goats break pens | the farmers start to talk; the guild calls a vote |
| **3. Breathing** | a plume by day, the hollow spreads, the spring boils | the Chapel's omens are undeniable; the road's north stretch cracks |
| **4. Fire** | lava fountains in the crater, glow at night, ash falls by day | the main road burns; only the tube, the path and the boats are left |
| **5. Eruption** | the playable climax (§7) | |

- **Time passes by play,** with shrine rests and days. A player who rests a lot pays for it.
- **The Wraith can buy time, at a cost to itself** (the chapter's quiet choice). At the crater it can **lend its own motion to the Warden's hold**: the cowl light dims, and one health thread is given up for good. Each gift holds back one stage for a day. It's the only way to save everyone in a slow game, and the Wraith carries the cost to the end. This also foreshadows the "become the stillness" ending (`storyline.md` §5).

## 7. The eruption (the climax, playable)

When the hold breaks, the mountain erupts:
- lava down the valleys the player has seen it fill toward;
- ash fall that buries tracks;
- a pyroclastic surge down the south flank, telegraphed by the Chapel's bells ringing on their own;
- the sky black at noon.

**The Ember Warden is released by the eruption itself, or by the Wraith.**
- The fight (BRIEF: aggressive, a duel, the caldera shrinking; `gameplay-design.md` §5) happens in the crater *while* the eruption runs.
- **Releasing it fast** tames the eruption: it settles into the caldera, and the flows slow.
- So the player has to decide: **fight now, or run one more rescue first.**

**The last rescues are play, not a cutscene.** Holdouts are still in their homes, and the Wraith can reach some of them:
- ash-skate down a flow's edge;
- Frost on the lava front makes obsidian, holding a flow at a pass while a cart gets through (BRIEF §4.4: ember construct + Frost becomes obsidian);
- a Ridge diverts a flow from a farmstead;
- Tide's steam clears a path through the surge's edge.

**There's time for some, not all.** The game never says which.

## 8. Afterwards

- **The eruption is seen.** Every village that didn't leave gets a cutscene of what happened to it: people thrown off their feet by the blast, swept into the lava, homes burning and falling. Then control returns in the ruin. Evacuated villages get the same mountain seen from the boats or the road. Design: `docs/people-and-cutscenes.md` §3.2.
- **The dead stay seen** (user: yes, 2026-11-22). Where a village didn't leave, the ash keeps their shapes. Each figure is frozen from its own last moment, with its own costume, so the player can tell who it was (`docs/people-and-cutscenes.md` §2). No text. The Remembrance page records each village by name and what became of it.
- **The living go somewhere real.**
  - Survivors make a new camp on the coast, which becomes part of the finale's gathering (`storyline.md` §5).
  - Some go to Hearn or the fen, and the player meets them again.
  - Their word about the Wraith travels with them: *"It came up the mountain for us"*, or *"It lied about the night"*.
- **The land.** The Waste is restored: crust breathing, ember storms (BRIEF §6.5). New black land is laid down by the flows the player shaped, and the obsidian walls the player made stay as permanent marks (pillar 2).
- **Ember is learned** whichever way it went. If the Wraith gave threads, it does so dimmer.

## 9. Design review (`wraith-design`)

- **The feature in one sentence:** an evacuation chapter where the player reads a volcano and wins four different peoples' trust before it erupts.
  - **Pillars:** 5, 2 and 3.
  - **Loop layers:** the session and the campaign.
  - **Aesthetics:** Narrative, Challenge and Discovery.
- **Isolation test:** NOT ASSESSED. The bare verbs (reading a sign with an element, talking, escorting) need a vertical slice: **one sign and one village (the Sow, the smallest)**, played by the user before anything else is built.
- **Smells:**
  - **Fetch in disguise:** a risk with carried evidence. Avoided because every sign is read with a verb, and proof works only on the right people.
  - **Complexity over depth:** a risk with four villages × belief × trust × stages. Each village has one decision-maker type and one proof type, and the state is two hidden values plus flags.
  - **Lying telegraph:** the bodies must show trust honestly. Bot-checked.
  - **Scope mismatch:** **present.** This is the largest chapter in the game. Fallback: three villages (fold the Sow into Cinderhold's lower terraces) if the slice shows it's too much.
  - **Dominant strategy:** "lie to everyone" is checked by word travelling between villages. "Give every thread" is checked by the cost staying on the body to the end.
- **The Ember Warden's brief:** to be written with the five axes when the fight is designed. Its tempo (a frantic duel during the eruption) and its arena (a caldera that shrinks) already differ from every other Warden.
- **Measure:**
  - the bot plays a "perfect" and a "careless" run; check the survivor counts differ and every village can be won in two ways;
  - the user plays the slice and says whether reading the people is fair;
  - frame time during the eruption, against 90 fps on the RX 9070 (ash, lava light, surge and crowds are heavy).

## 10. Build notes

- **New kits, not Hearn's:** basalt galleries and lifts (Cinderhold), pumice cells and ribbons (the Chapel), dry-stone farmsteads and terraces (the Sow), stilts, boats and jetty (Steamhaven). Each has its own materials in `data/materials/` per the art bar.
- **People:** named decision-makers as full Veiled figures, with each village's dress and dyes. The crowds use the instanced crowd renderer (`render/crowd.js`), not dozens of cloth sims (M0's cloth lesson).
- **State:** per village `belief`, `trust`, `stage left at`, `count saved`, and flags for promises, lies, the tithe, Annick and Aled. It is saved and read by barks, placement, the ending, and the people who travel (`storyline.md` §4).
- **Order:** the slice (one sign, the Sow) → the climb and the crater → Steamhaven → Cinderhold → the Chapel → the clock → the eruption → afterwards.

## 12. The slice, built (2026-11-23)

- **The volcano:** three CC0 scans on the cone (`data/materials/sources.json` 35–37: ambientCG Rock035 as the obsidian, Lava001's crust with its emission in the height map's blue, Poly Haven burned_ground_01 as ash), `shaders/volcano.wgsl.js`. The seams glow near the crater, strongest at night. The rest of the ember biome has ground now too (soil, dry grass, ash toward the cone, rock on the steeps), no longer clay.
- **The Sow** (`world/sow.js`, `systems/sow.js`, `data/quests/ember.json`): six farmsteads down the valley west of the cone, each a vaulted house, byre, cistern or well, a yard wall of rubble, a threshing floor, a drying rack and three staked terraces. Heights come from `data/world/sowHeights.json` (`tools/world/sowHeights.mjs`, the exact CPU ground). Twelve costumed farmers, two per family, each family in its own dyes. Grass grows in the valley, autumn-dry, with none in the yards.
- **Play:**
  - Cole warns the Wraith at the Cinder Shrine.
  - Crystallize on the new steam fissures sets `ember:steam-read`. The farmers dismiss it as "the mountain's business".
  - Each farm's sign:
    - Aled's warm trough and Mari's warm terrace: Crystallize on them;
    - Gwil's well: Draw the bucket;
    - Bethan's cistern, Huw's floor, Ffion's byre: Look at them.
  - Once found, the elder can be shown the sign. They walk to it with the Wraith and believe.
  - Each elder waits for neighbours on the road first (`NEED`).
  - Once ready, the Wraith chooses for them:
    - help load (two hours pass);
    - or leave the harvest (one of the family stays).
  - Aled goes only on a promise for his flock.
  - The lie sends a family at once. At the next dawn every family still home hears of it, and needs two more neighbours gone.
  - The farm shows each state: bundles out, cart loaded, the door left open.
- **Verified:** `tools/bot/sow-run.mjs` plays warning → steam → Bethan (shown, loaded) → Huw (shown once Bethan was on the road, left light) → the lie at Ffion's → found at dawn. 90 fps audit: Sow and crater spots p95 8.4 ms.
- **The headland camp** (`world/sow.js` CAMP, about (720, −2640)): the coast below the valley is a 250 m cliff, so the road goes up the valley's head to a clearing on the headland above the sea. It has a fire ring, three shelters and bundles, and appears once the first family leaves. Families walk the whole road (faster when out of sight) and stand round the fire.
- **The clock** (`systems/emberClock.js`): after Cole's warning, each dawn moves the mountain on one stage, holding at stage 4, Fire. A notice announces each new stage, and tremors (shake and rumble) come more often as it rises. The plume grows from small and pale while the mountain sleeps to a tall black column lit from below at Fire. From stage 2 on, each stage makes every Sow family need one neighbour fewer on the road. At the summit the Wraith can give a health thread for good (interact twice); each thread holds the mountain back one stage and dims the cowl light. The summit offers it only while it would actually hold the mountain back.
- **All six farms were played** (bot plus live checks): every sign reads, the neighbour cascade works (after Mari and Aled left, Gwil became ready), and `ember-waking` completes once every family has decided.
- **Test spots:** `?spot=e1-cole` and `?spot=e1-sow`.
- **Cinderhold** (`world/cinderhold.js`, `systems/cinderhold.js`): built on a bench of the cone's west flank at about 540 m, above the Sow. The east plateau first chosen turned out to be dune biome. The town is basalt and iron:
  - three gallery mouths in the face, their insides lamplit;
  - the deep shaft under an iron headframe, with the tithe cage on its chain;
  - rails and carts from the middle gallery to the spoil heap;
  - the guild hall, four miners' houses, and lamps day and night.
  - New building material, `MAT.BASALT`, on the rock-strata scan; exact heights in `data/world/cinderHeights.json`.
  - Seven people in a new soot-grey `cinder` costume; the three masters wear oxblood shawls.
- **How Cinderhold plays:** the guild votes, two of three masters.
  - Teg votes to go once shown the bell-thermometer in the north gallery.
  - Vell votes to go once three Sow families are on the road ("no figs, no bread") or the mountain reaches Breathing.
  - Morcant votes yes only on a deal: the Wraith keeps the tithe secret, the lower tube opens, and the cage goes down one last time.
  - With two votes, everyone leaves by the lower tube: they walk into the south gallery and reappear at the headland camp.
  - Or the Wraith shows Bron the cage. Its board of debtors' names includes Hal Tanner, Cole's father. The miners bring the debtors up (Cole has a line for it) and leave overland, the masters stay, and the guild seals the tube.
  - Played by `tools/bot/cinder-run.mjs vote|expose`; the deal path was checked live.
- **The Ash Chapel** (`world/ashChapel.js`, `systems/ashChapel.js`): on a bench of the south-west flank at about 710 m, 235 m from the crater.
  - **Buildings:** five pumice cells in the face, a whitewashed domed chapel with its bronze bell, prayer ribbons, the sacred spring in a grotto, and the scripture stele. The still hollow, where the birds lie, is at the summit's ring.
  - **People:** Annick the Seer and five faithful (Nerys, Bryok, Lio, Elowen, Idris) in a new pale `ash` dress.
  - **The scripture** names three omens, and its end says go down and do not look back. The omens:
    - the soured spring (Taste it);
    - the birds in the still hollow (Look);
    - the mountain breathing by day (stage 3).
  - **Reading Nerys the end** takes the scripture plus two omens. The faithful then go down by their own path round the north flank (`ember:path-known`) to the camp.
  - **The bell rings every in-game hour.** Speaking while it rings is rude, and the faithful then want all three omens.
  - **Annick never goes willingly.** The Wraith can leave her, promise to stand with her at the spring, or carry her off by force. Force means she never forgives, and every Sow family not yet gone wants one more neighbour on the road.
  - Played by `tools/bot/chapel-run.mjs force|promise|leave`. Five names were changed because they clashed with existing characters.
- **Two performance bugs found by the 90 fps audit:**
  - **Distant figures were drawn.** For a figure far away on known ground, the shown test gave `undefined`, and `setEnabled(undefined)` leaves a Babylon mesh enabled. Every distant figure drew its cloth and shadow from across the world. Fixed in `systems/npcs.js`.
  - **Cloth slots ran out.** The GPU cloth solver had 64 slots. The 26 Ember people filled them, and the meadow's last-made folk fell back to CPU cloth at 0.7 ms each. Raised to 128 slots.
