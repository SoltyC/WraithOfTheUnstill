# Storylines: Ebbhold and the set pieces (proposal, 2026-11-01)

The user's brief: an abandoned city under the water; the people in it died, and the Drowned are those people; a stranger on a jetty gives the Wraith a breathing apparatus with limited air; on the way down it tears on the kelp, so the only way out is deeper; a whirlpool of bubbles gives air but pulls the Wraith into a hidden cave.

**The shape of these stories:** a kindness that turns out to be a trap, a point of no return the player feels with their own hands, and a truth at the bottom that changes what the beginning meant. Each one below follows it. Names are working names.

Dialogue follows `docs/village/dialogue-style.md`: plain words from people under strain.

---

## Why does a thing with no lungs need air?

The Wraith is an empty robe holding the world's last motion. It doesn't breathe. So the air is not for breathing:

**Deep water is the stillest place left. It puts out whatever moves.** Below the surface, the stillness presses in on the cowl light. The apparatus, a Shaper device called **the Lung**, holds moving air against the Wraith's chest. As long as it turns over, the motion in the robe stays lit.

This gives the game its gauge without a HUD (BRIEF: state on the body):
- The cowl light shrinks as the Lung empties.
- The bubbles thin, then stop.
- The robe, which floats and billows at full air, starts to hang still.

*Morrow: "You don't breathe. I know. The water doesn't care. It puts out anything that moves."*

## The main storyline: The Lung

**Where:** the fen ends at a black round water, **the Sounding**, a flooded pit 500 m across near the coast. A rotted jetty runs out over it. Under it lies **Ebbhold**.

**The city:** the Shapers turned the tides from here. The Veiled who served them lived in it, a whole people, under great sea-doors that held the water out. When the world began to still, the Listener was set here to keep the tide asleep. It did what it was told. It silenced everything. The doors stopped turning, and the water came in.

### 1. The jetty
At the end of the jetty sits **Morrow**, a Veiled diver with a lantern and a pile of brass.

**The player must love Morrow before this.** Morrow is the fen's guide for the whole first half of the chapter: teaches the quiet walk, shares a fire, jokes about the Wraith having no feet, saves it once from the Mudlings. This takes hours, not a conversation. Then, finally, the ask: *"My sister's down there. She was on the doors. I can't go down again. You can."* Morrow fits the Lung to the Wraith's chest with shaking hands and warns: *"You've air to go down and come back. No more. Don't stop to look."*

### 2. The descent
It's the most beautiful moment in the phase. The light goes green, then blue. The robe spreads. Towers show far below. Then the kelp forest, tall as the mangroves above it.

### 3. The tear
Brushing the kelp, the Lung catches. A hiss, a stream of silver bubbles from the Wraith's chest. **The player does this: it happens as they pass through, wherever they choose to go.** The light starts to shrink quickly.

### 4. No way up
The surface is a pale coin far above. The player will try for it. They should: that's the moment that teaches them. The light fades too fast, and the robe sags and sinks. **They don't die:** they wake lower, on a ledge among the kelp roots, with a thread of air left. Going down now is their own decision.

Below, there's a glow: a pillar of bubbles rising out of the dark.

### 5. The breath of Ebbhold
The bubbles refill the Lung, and the light comes back, a huge relief. Then the player notices they're turning. The column is a whirlpool, the city's old intake, and it's dragging them down. They can fight it and hold on for a while, but it wins. **Bubbles fill the screen.**

### 6. The undercroft
The Wraith wakes in a hidden cave, in air, on black sand, under the city. The Lung is finished. There's no way back up. The tunnel runs one way: into Ebbhold.

### 7. The city
Dry halls and flooded ones, joined by old doors. And the Drowned: that's what Morrow calls them, and what the player will call them too.

They keep doing what they were doing: one kneads bread, one rocks a cradle, one sweeps a stair. Their veils float round their heads. They don't see, but a noise turns every head in the room, and they come, hands out, and hold on to the robe.

### 8. The register
In the Tide Hall, a stone book names everyone who lived here. Saying a name to one of the Drowned stops them: they put down what they're holding and go still for good. It feels like mercy. Morrow told the player it would be. **The player does this as many times as they like, believing they're being kind.**

### 9. Morrow
Morrow's sister, still at her wheel, has a torn Lung beside her and Morrow's name carved on it. The player assumes the obvious: Morrow is dead too. It's a false bottom, and it's the sad ending the player expects. They carry on, moved, sure they've understood the story.

### 10. The Listener
It wakes and lends Tide. The fight is the biggest of the game so far, ending with one long Draw and the Undertow. The sea-doors turn. The Drowned lift their heads. **Music swells. The game gives the player the same triumph as every release before it.**

**How the truth is told:** `release-cinematic.md`. The player holds the key through the flashbacks that show it, and letting go doesn't stop it.

### 11. The morning
The Wraith surfaces at dawn, into the signature frame: mirror water, mist-hung trees. It's the most beautiful thing in the game. Then the camera settles, and the player sees what's on the water.

**Veils. Thousands of them, floating up, spreading across the fen.**

Morrow is on the jetty, alive, and not surprised.

## The betrayal

**The Drowned were not dead.**

Ebbhold's flood was the tide, and motion was what killed the city. The Listener wasn't its gaoler; it was its rescue. In the hour the water came in, it stopped everything, and held every person in that moment, alive, between one breath and the next. It has held them for forty years, waiting for someone to come and open the doors.

**That's what "held in stillness" means.** Each Warden is keeping something from happening.

The Wraith came, and everything it did reads differently now:

| What the player saw | What it was |
|---|---|
| The Drowned swarm towards any noise | They were waking. They heard someone at last |
| Hands on the robe, dragging it back | They were begging it to stop |
| The Listener hunting every sound | It was protecting them: noise breaks the hold |
| The bread, the cradle | Not a ghost's habit. The same second, still happening |
| Saying a name "stops them for good" | **The player was killing them, one at a time, thinking it was mercy** |
| The release, with the music | Everyone still held drowned, all at once, in the hour they'd been saved from |

### Who betrayed the Wraith

**Morrow,** alone (user: Aud is out; most players will have forgotten the keeper by now). Everything rests on the player loving her first: she's their guide, friend and rescuer through the whole fen.

She knew. There was no accident: she cut the Lung so that the Wraith couldn't turn back. She told it to say the names. Her sister was alive down there the whole time, and Morrow stood at that well three times and couldn't do it herself.

*"I cut the Lung. I told you to say the names. You'd never have gone all the way if you'd known. That's why it had to be you."*

**It's a defensible choice.** That's why it stays with the player. Liese has been one breath from drowning for forty years, and nothing was ever going to come. The fen is alive, the tide is back. For the first time in the game, the world looks better and feels worse.

The full scene is in `voice-script.md`, section 9.

### Did the player do anything right?

**Some people lived.** The ones still held in **rooms with their doors shut** survive: the sea came in only where it could reach them. Every door the player forced, every room drained or flooded on the way through, every name said, changed that number. Nobody told them. Quiet, careful players saved more without knowing why.

That night the survivors come ashore, and the camp counts them. **The count is the chapter's real ending,** and it can't be redone without replaying the city. Some will look at the Wraith. None will speak to it.

The rule from `quest-template.md` holds: nothing fails; a missed moment changes the outcome.

### What stays changed

The twist has to change play, or it was only a cutscene.

- **The Veiled stop kneeling.** The pilgrims who revered the Wraith turn away as it passes. Barks change everywhere. The Wraith is feared now, not revered.
- **The fen's people won't trade with it** until the survivors' quest (below) is done.
- **The robe keeps the water.** Its hem drips for the rest of the game, and leaves wet footprints in every biome: a permanent mark on the terrain state.
- **The cowl light is a shade dimmer.** A small thing the player will notice is gone.
- **The Remembrance page** gains the names of everyone the player named. All of them.
- **Morrow stays on the jetty.** The lantern stays lit. She can be found there, reading the names, for the rest of the game.

### The rest of the game

- **The next release isn't the same.** The Ember Warden is reached by a player who now knows that stopping might be the kind thing and that the release might be a crime. **That doubt is the point.** Before it, the player can go looking for what that Warden is holding.
- **The sealed library** in the frost (Ember key) holds the last Shaper's journal, addressed to the Wraith: *"Do not let them send you to the water."* Too late, and the player will know it.
- **The ending gains its meaning.** "The Wraith settles into the tide" stops being a poetic exit. It goes into the water that it filled. Not a victory: a debt paid.
- **A survivors' quest:** *The Register.* Read the names aloud at the jetty, with the survivors. It doesn't fix anything, but it's what's left to do.

### Keeping it fair

A twist only works if the second look shows it was all there. Plant these, and say nothing:

- Candle flames in the city are lit, and don't flicker.
- The bread on the table is fresh. The ink in the register is wet.
- When a name is said, the person's mouth moves. The sound is under the water, so the player can't quite hear it. In the final replay it's a word: *"Don't."*
- The Listener never kills a Drowned, and turns away from them when it strikes.
- Morrow never says they're dead. Only "gone down", "stayed down", "she's waiting".
- At the jetty Morrow's hands shake while fitting the Lung, and they're the hands of someone doing something terrible.
- At the fire she asks whether things stop when the Wraith finishes with them, and says "Good" a little too steadily.

**Don't tell the player there's a twist, and don't hint with music.** It must play straight, right up to the veils.

### Alternatives

**1. The Listener is on your side.** The same reveal, happening **before** the release: the player learns the truth at the well, and has to choose. Cleaner morally and more player choice, but they feel torn, not betrayed. This one is the fallback if the main version proves too bleak.

**2. The Wraith did it.** A smaller change: the flood that drowned Ebbhold's last survivors is the meltwater from the Frost Warden's release. The player killed them a chapter ago, with the game's first victory. It's sharp, but without Morrow and without the names it's a fact to absorb, not a wound.

## More storylines in this shape

### The Mire

**The Ferry.** A raft waits in the fog with a silent poler who takes the Wraith where it asks, for free. Each trip the fog is thicker and the poler a little closer. Then the player sees the raft has no reflection. It's been carrying them in a circle round something that is slowly rising under them. *Way out:* step off into the dark water and read the ripples home. *Twist:* the poler is trying to warn them, and is pointing, not poling.

**The Last Air.** Knocking comes through a wall in a flooded quarter. Behind it there's a sealed room still holding air, and in it an old woman who has lived on sealed food and rainwater for forty years, and doesn't know it's over. Opening the door floods the room. *The play:* use Draw to drain the corridor first, then Crystallize the leak, and do it quietly. *Choice:* bring her up, or let her stay. She asks to stay. *Consequence:* a light in that window, seen from the roofs at low tide.

**The Bell in the Mud.** Something rings in the fen at dusk. Villagers' dogs walk to it and don't return. It's a Shaper bell sunk in mud, and each ring wakes Mudlings. Silencing it means going under it, at the Lung's limit. *Twist:* the bell is what has kept the worst of them asleep, and stopping it has a cost for the camp.

### The Frost

**The Library Under the Ice** (the ice-sealed library in `cross-biome`). A pilgrim asks the Wraith to test a frozen lake for a crossing. It breaks. The Wraith falls into a sealed hall that the lake has been the roof of, and the only way out is the last Shaper's own stair. *Twist:* the journal there is addressed to the Wraith, which hadn't existed yet. **Quest content only: the Warden fight stays as it is.**

### The Dunes

**The Buried Door** (fills a Phase 9 stand-in). Pell's kite snags on a pole in the sand. Pull it, and the sand starts running like an hourglass, and the Wraith with it. Falling, they land in a chamber that's slowly filling. *The play:* Stone and Frost to climb the falling sand. *Twist:* it's a clock. The Shapers buried a clock under the dunes that counts down to the world stopping, and it has nine days left, and has had nine days left for a thousand years.

### The Ember Waste

**The Cage.** Miners at a lift offer to lower the Wraith to a lost seam. The cable burns through halfway. The cage falls and sticks on a ledge over the lava tube, swinging, heating. *Way out:* down, across cooling crust, through a tube that's filling. *Twist:* the miners cut it, as they've been paying a tithe to something below, and the Wraith was the tithe. Later, one of them comes down after it, ashamed.

### The Coast

**The Keeper's Light.** A lighthouse on a stack keeps turning with no keeper. Climb it and the stair goes on longer than the tower is tall. The tide comes up inside it behind the Wraith. *Twist:* the keeper has been dead for an age; the light points out to sea, to show the last Warden where the land is. Putting it out is the only way to make it come ashore: the start of the finale.

## How they connect

- **Every biome keeps one person who can't stop:** the keeper at the light, the miner's guilt. Morrow is the one who stopped, and paid someone else to.
- **After Ebbhold, every set piece reads as a warning:** the player will look at the clock under the dunes and the light on the coast and wonder what each one is holding back.
- **Morrow's Lung returns.** A repaired one gives limited dives anywhere after the release, opening underwater nooks in the meadow's lakes and on the coast: new use for old places (pillar 2).
- **The Listener's noise rule** runs through the fen, the city and the fight, so it's taught three times before it's tested.
