# The Listener's release: making the player understand (design, 2026-11-01)

User's direction: the player must actually realise what happened, not shrug it off. The gameplay has to carry the story. Freeing the Warden plays a cutscene that makes it all obvious, with animated flashbacks, ending on the floating robes of the dead on the surface.

Story: `storylines.md`. Work order: `plan.md`, U6 and U7.

---

## The rule

**The player must be able to say, unprompted: "They were alive, and I killed them."**

Mood and hints aren't enough. Five things have to land, in order, and each has its own proof on screen:

| # | The player must understand | Shown by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | These were people | the city alive, the same people they walked past |
| 2 | The Listener saved them | the moment it stops the wave |
| 3 | They were alive all along | the frozen crowd in the exact poses they saw |
| 4 | I was betrayed | Morrow's knife on the Lung; Aud on the jetty |
| 5 | **I did this** | the player's own actions, replayed |

**The test:** after the chapter, ask a player who hasn't read this: what happened, and who did it? If they can't say, the sequence has failed. Fix it before anything else in the phase.

Why players shrug things off, and the answer to each:

| Why it fails | Answer |
|---|---|
| "They were just enemies" | They're individuals with names, routines and one child beside you (*Before*) |
| "A cutscene happened to someone else" | It uses their own actions, and their own hand starts it |
| "I didn't choose this" | They said the names; the count depends on them |
| "It's over, on to the next thing" | Control comes back in the water, slowly, with no way to rush (*After*) |
| "It's only sad music" | Everything in play changes, and stays changed |

## Before: making them people

The Drowned have to be the best-animated thing in the chapter, because everything rests on them.

**Five people the player will remember,** each with a readable loop, a room and a line in the register. Everyone else is a variation on them.

| Person | Doing | Found out from the register |
|---|---|---|
| **Hanne** | kneading bread | fed the door crews through the last night |
| **Teodor and the cradle** | rocking | a father; the baby was born that week |
| **Old Ise** | sweeping the stair | sang while she worked, so the children could find her |
| **The door crew** | hauling a wheel | Morrow's sister, Liese, and four others |
| **Sefa** | see below | |

**Quality bar:** from 5 m, with no text, a viewer can say what each one is doing. This needs real hand and body animation on the robe system, not idle sway. **It's a user checkpoint on footage.** If the loops don't read, the flashback's match cuts don't either.

### Sefa

A child of about seven. She's the one person who doesn't swarm. She takes hold of the Wraith's hem in the first dry hall, and walks behind it, silent. **Nothing in the game explains why.** She's the first person in years to come near the Wraith without kneeling.

- **She's useful:** she knows the way, tugs towards safe doors, and freezes when the Listener is near. A real help, so the player keeps her close.
- **She's quiet:** she makes the player quieter, because they don't want to lose her.
- **She can't be named:** her name isn't in the register, since children weren't entered until seven. It's a small thing the player can only understand later.
- **At the last door she stops,** and won't go further. She lets go. The player goes on alone. **If that door stays shut behind them, she lives.** Nothing says so.

The player's care for her is what the whole chapter cashes in. She must be built and tuned before the cutscene is.

### The clues, played

Each clue is something the player *does*, so they remember it:
- A candle: try to blow it out with Gust. The flame doesn't move, though the player's own light does.
- Bread on a table: pick it up. It's warm. (Item text, plain: "Warm.")
- The register: wipe a line with a sleeve. The ink comes off.
- Naming: the person's mouth moves, and the sound is too low to read. The subtitle shows only *"…"*.

## The climax: the player's hand does it

The release isn't a button press followed by a film. **The player holds the key through it.**

1. **The Last Draw.** The Listener is stranded. The player holds Draw. All the water in the well rises, the biggest thing in the game, and the music builds.
2. **The Undertow prompt:** hold Heavy to send it down. There's no other option. The player holds.
3. **As they hold, the flashback begins**, bleeding in over the water, with the music still rising. The first shots look like a reward: the city alive and golden. The player thinks this is the Warden's thanks.
4. **At the wave, the music stops.**
5. **The player will try to let go.** That's the moment. When the frozen crowd is understood, **they can release the key, and it doesn't stop.** The Wraith's arm stays out. The controller does nothing. *The game keeps doing what they told it to.*

That's the feeling of betrayal in the hands, not in a story. It costs little to build, and it's the point of the sequence.

## The cutscene: shot list

About 3 minutes. In-engine, in the real rooms, letterboxed. **The first viewing can't be skipped.** (Today Jump and Interact skip every release, and a player mashing Space in the climax would lose the whole chapter. Replays later can be skipped.)

No spoken voices; the game has none. Plain subtitles only where marked.

**Grade:** the past is warm and sunlit. The held age is green-blue and still. The present is the true colour of the fight.

| # | Time | Shot | Proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 | From the well, the water starts to fall. Warm light floods up into it. | |
| 2 | 0:08 | **Match cut:** Hanne's loop as the player saw it, then the same framing, dry and sunlit, the bread real. Teodor: the cradle has a baby in it. Old Ise, singing, with children on the stair. | 1 |
| 3 | 0:25 | Wide: the great hall alive, hundreds of people, sun through the sea-doors. Liese laughing at the wheel. Morrow beside her, young, handing her a Lung. | 1 |
| 4 | 0:38 | Sefa running up the stair towards her father. A shudder. Dust falls. Everyone looks up. | |
| 5 | 0:44 | The sea-doors crack. **The music cuts.** Water comes down the hall as a wall. People run. | |
| 6 | 0:52 | Close: the wave over Sefa, inches from her raised hand. | |
| 7 | 0:54 | From the well, the Listener rises, and opens its arms. **Everything stops.** Water, spray, a falling cup. Silence. | 2 |
| 8 | 1:00 | The camera moves through the frozen crowd, in the **exact poses the player has walked past**. Hanne's hands in the dough. Teodor at the cradle. It reaches Sefa's hand, and the eyes behind her veil **move**. | 3 |
| 9 | 1:15 | Time passes over them: the green comes in, weed grows, the light changes. The flames don't move. The Listener sits by the well, listening. Subtitle: *They waited.* | 3 |

**The robes look like the Wraith.** Empty cloth, face down, arms spread. The player has spent the game as one of these. Nobody needs to say so.

**How the deaths are shown:** never directly. No bodies, no suffering on screen; the cut to black does the work, and the robes are empty. This is a mythic game, and restraint hurts more.

## After: making them stay in it

**Control returns in the water, among the robes, not at a menu.**

1. **The player is standing up to the chest in the Sounding's shallows,** a long way from the jetty.
2. **Nothing works.** RMB gives no skim: the water no longer carries the Wraith. Sprint and spells do nothing. The robes part round the body with a soft drag. They have to **walk**, about 90 seconds, and they have to push through them. The ripple simulation (F1) moves them.
3. **No music.** Only water.
4. **Robes can be looked at.** Interact on one shows a line from the register, and if the player named them, it says so: *"Hanne. You said her name."*
5. **The one objective in the HUD, in the game's plain style:** *"Walk to the jetty."*

### The jetty

Morrow and Aud, waiting. Morrow's line, then Aud's (`storylines.md`). **Plain facts, per the dialogue rules:** *"They were alive. All of them. You knew that by the end, didn't you."*

The only choice is what to do with the Lung still on the Wraith's chest: hand it to Morrow, or drop it in the water. It changes one thing later.

### That night

The camp on the shore. The count of survivors, in plain text. **If Sefa lived,** she's there, and when the Wraith comes near she moves behind someone. **If she didn't,** a small robe has been brought ashore, and the camp won't let the Wraith near it.

### The quest log

The player's own journal changes. Every *Done* in the chapter is rewritten, in place:

- ~~"Lay the Drowned to rest"~~ → **"You killed 37 people."** The true number.
- ~~"Free the Warden"~~ → **"You ended Ebbhold."**

A plain sentence in the player's own log is hard to shrug off. It stays there for the rest of the game.

### From then on

Everything in `storylines.md`, *What stays changed*, and:

- **The title screen changes.** On any save past this point, the opening shot is the robes on the water at dawn. They see it each time they start the game.
- **The next rest at a shrine:** a dream with no words: the frozen crowd, now gone.
- **The fen stays silent** until the survivors' quest is finished: no music there at all.
- **The first time a Veiled pilgrim turns away,** play it where the player can't miss it: Tobin, or whoever the player has helped most.

## Building it

### What exists

Today's release cinematics are one hand-written camera (`releaseCam.js`, `longWalker.js`) with letterbox bars, a depth-of-field focus and a Jump/Interact skip. There's no shot list, no crowd, no flashback.

### What's needed

**A cinematic director** (`src/systems/cine/`). A timeline from data (`data/cine/listener-release.json`): shots, cuts, camera paths, actors, grade, subtitles, sound. It also serves every later cutscene. Allocation-free at runtime, like the rest.

**The stage: the same rooms, in another state.** U4 already builds the city in dry and flooded versions. The past is its dry state, with warm light. **Flashbacks are the real rooms, so the player recognises them.** Everything is warmed at load: 0 late pipelines through the whole sequence, or the cut stutters at the worst moment.

**Actors:**
- 5–8 hero people per shot with full robe cloth and hand animation.
- Crowds up to a few hundred as baked, vertex-animated robes.
- The frozen state is the same clip, stopped.

**The frozen wave:** a sculpted water mesh sharing the F1 water shading, plus held droplets. A bespoke piece.

**Your record.** The real thing, not a stand-in:
- Whenever a name is said, save the person's id, the place, and the last 4 seconds of the Wraith's position and facing.
- Saved in the save file (a few hundred bytes each).
- Shot 12 reads this list and replays the Wraith with the existing feed override (`wraith.js`), from a camera at the held person's eye. **At most 12 shown in full, then the rest as a fast cut of names.**
- **Having named no one:** shot 12 shows the doors the Wraith forced, the rooms it flooded and the people dragged off it, from the same list. A pacifist still ended the city, and the sequence still works.

**Floating robes:** one instanced cloth mesh, up to about 4,000, moved by the ripple field; 20–30 real cloth robes near the camera. **Budget:** the signature shot's water, plus at most 1 ms. Low gets fewer, with the same frame.

**The held key:** Undertow's input goes on reading the hold, and stops affecting anything. Small, and essential.

**Sound:** the music cut, the single silence and the sound-only drowning. The music is the user's, and these cues need it.

**Chapter state saved:** every flag above, plus the rewritten journal, the survivors' count and the name record. The title screen reads the flag.

### Tests and review

- A `tools/bot/` playthrough with three records: named none, named a few, named everyone. Every branch finishes.
- `reactions` and quest validators stay green; save, reload and load across the jetty.
- **Filmed with the existing `film` tools for review before play.**
- **User checkpoints, in order:** the Drowned's loops; Sefa alone; a rough cut with grey rooms; the real thing.
- **The comprehension test** (top of this file) on a player who hasn't read any of this.

The rough cut is the important one. **If a grey version doesn't make the point, no polish will.** Build it as soon as U4's rooms exist, before the art.

### Risks

- **Quality is the whole bet.** Stiff animation or a stuttering cut turns this into something embarrassing. It's the highest bar in the game so far.
- **No voices:** all of this is picture, sound and a few words. Silent shots must be very clear.
- **The child:** she's the emotional centre, and it's easy to overdo. No shot shows her in harm. Her ending stays small.
- **Too long, too hard:** the walk through the robes must be just long enough. Test it at 60, 90 and 120 seconds.
- **Cut scope last here.** If the phase runs long, cut side quests and sites before any of this: it's the reason for the chapter.
