# Design Wraith around verbs, not content

**Build "The Wraith of the Unstill" as a small set of pleasurable, element-modified verbs (carve, glide, climb, grip, bend, leave a mark) that obey one auditable surface-by-element rule table, and make every Warden a different kind of problem rather than a bigger copy of the last one.** The notes support this from several directions. Breath of the Wild's designers describe replacing bespoke puzzles with interacting rules. Ueda's Shadow of the Colossus team varied the discovery problem and arena, not the player's toolset. Miyazaki and Chen disagree on difficulty selectors but both reject hidden scaling, which fits your no-scaling rule. For the Long Walker, the strongest differentiator from the static frost mammoth (freeze and shatter) and the manor-siege Bellwether (strip vents, climb the neck, work three valves) is a moving-target win condition: read its gait, shape the sand under its feet, stumble a leg, and only then climb. Two caveats apply to everything below. First, most primary talks (GDC Vault, the MDA paper, Chen's thesis) were paywalled or undecodable in the research session, so many "primary" items are developer statements read through interviews or press recaps. Second, the specific application to Wraith is my inference throughout. On Claude skills: nothing official exists, and the only packs worth reading are saschb2b's `game-design` and the `game-designer` agent and `/balance-check` skill inside Claude-Code-Game-Studios, ideally adapted into a small project skill.

**How claims are labelled.** **[P]** means a developer or designer statement retrieved from an interview, dev blog or repo file (primary in origin, though often republished). **[S]** means secondhand: an analyst, reviewer, blogger or press summary of a talk or book. **[I]** means my inference or application, unsourced. Items the notes recalled from memory but could not verify are left out of the body.

## Motivation frameworks tell you what to keep and what to cut

Start from the felt experience, not the feature list. The MDA framework separates mechanics (rules), dynamics (run-time behaviour) and aesthetics (emotional response), and notes that designers touch only mechanics while players experience aesthetics first **[S]** ([Wikipedia: MDA framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDA_framework)). The eight aesthetics it names are Sensation, Fantasy, Narrative, Challenge, Fellowship, Discovery, Expression and Submission. For Wraith the target set is Sensation, Discovery, Fantasy and Submission, with Challenge concentrated in Warden fights and Fellowship deliberately absent **[I]**. This gives a cheap veto test: a feature that serves none of those aesthetics (loot, levels, a quest log) is cut. Terrain memory is the standout mechanic under this lens because one system yields Expression (you leave a signature on the world) and Fantasy (the world is alive) **[I]**.

Self-determination theory supplies the case that loot is unnecessary. Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski found across four studies that felt autonomy and competence predicted enjoyment, game preference and wellbeing, and in an online multiplayer community relatedness also predicted enjoyment and future play **[P, correlational self-report]** ([ACU research bank](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8q128/the-motivational-pull-of-video-games-a-self-determination-theory-approach); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2006/12/061226134706.htm)). Intuitive controls and presence also tracked these needs, which supports putting game feel first. Quantic Foundry's model, built on more than 1.25 million gamers, describes Discovery as "explore, tinker, experiment" and links Immersion to story and fantasy **[S]** ([GDC 2020 slides](https://quanticfoundry.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/GDC-2020-Slides-Player-Segments-Quantic-Foundry.pdf)). Wraith's likely audience is the Discovery, Immersion and mastery-Challenge cluster **[I]**. Miyazaki names the same two rewards, "sense of achievement" and "surprise of discovery," as the core of Dark Souls **[P]** ([Game Informer 2011](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2011/11/12/afterwords-dark-souls)).

Applied to your constraints, competence without levels should be shown through the world: a line you could not surf before is now clean, and your groove trail shows it **[I]**. Autonomy means a free Warden order, several valid climbs, and no gate that says "come back later" **[I]**. Relatedness comes from the Wardens themselves, whose patterns and grief the player learns, rather than from NPC count **[I]**. Reward without loot then has six sources, all inferred: discovery of beautiful or faster hidden lines; knowledge (Warden patterns and wind rules that open options without gating); mastery feedback via your own trail; restoration, where each release visibly changes the biome; cosmetics earned by acts as Expression; and anticipation, with a Warden silhouette on the horizon long before it can be climbed **[I]**. Breath of the Wild supplies one caution about discovery rewards. Fujibayashi's blog says experimental rocks to look under were not rewarding alone, so Koroks were hidden beneath them **[P]** ([DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/breath-of-the-wild-korok-seeds/)). Any Wraith "look here" spot needs a small payoff, such as a gag, a melancholy beat or a view.

## Loops, flow and pacing: fixed difficulty, player-chosen challenge

No retrievable source on nested core loops was read, so the Wraith loop stack is my proposal **[I]**: a 0-10 second loop where carving, surfing and gliding are pleasurable alone (test: is moving fun in an empty field?); a minute loop of reading terrain, choosing a line and chaining modes to leave a mark; a 5-15 minute loop of spotting a landmark, travelling and finding a micro-site that teaches an element; a session loop of approaching and releasing one Warden so the biome visibly softens; and a campaign loop of elements and world-state accumulation. The one sourced nuance is from Halo. Griesemer says the popular "30 seconds of fun" reading is backwards: "No 30 second stretch of Halo is ever repeated," because context keeps changing, nested as a 3-second loop in a 30-second loop in a 3-minute loop **[P]** ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-07-14-half-minute-halo-an-interview-with-jaime-griesemer.html)). The lesson is to vary the surface and situation around fixed verbs, not to add verbs.

On difficulty, two defensible camps agree that automatic scaling is wrong. Chen argues passive dynamic difficulty adjustment fails because games cannot read flow, performance does not reliably track enjoyment, and fixed designer assumptions do not generalise. He proposes player-chosen difficulty embedded in core mechanics and a wide variety of experiences to widen the flow zone **[P/S]** ([Chen's design page](https://www.jenovachen.com/flowingames/designfig.htm)). Miyazaki rejects selectors because he wants everyone "to the same level of discussion and the same level of enjoyment" **[P, secondhand coverage]** ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/e3-2018-from-software-explains-why-dark-souls-bloo/1100-6459827/)), and says easing Elden Ring would "break the game" ([VideoGamer](https://videogamer.com/news/miyazaki-says-making-elden-ring-more-accessible-and-easier-would-break-the-game)). He also admitted Elden Ring "went a little too far" with poison swamps **[S]** ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/elden-ring-creator-difficulty/)). Combining them is consistent with your design: one fixed world, with difficulty expressed as route and risk choice (steeper ridge for a faster line, which Warden first) **[I]**. Add optional assist settings kept separate from "difficulty," generous checkpoints at phase boundaries, and no loss of world marks on failure **[I]**.

For pacing, the notes support deliberate quiet. Ueda's long empty rides between colossi function as reflection and pacing **[S]** ([Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/de/blog/design-philosophy-fumito-ueda), a single opinion essay). The Left 4 Dead Director settles action after intense stretches, a relax-build-peak-relax rhythm **[S]** ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/making-left-4-dead)). For Wraith, that translates to an intensity budget with no hostile spawns after a release, and ambience (music swells on vista reveals) doing the pacing rather than a spawning director **[I]**. Use Schell's lenses (Curiosity, Surprise, Problem Solving, Endogenous Value, Essential Experience) as per-area review prompts **[S]** ([CIRCA wiki](https://circa.cs.ualberta.ca/index.php/CIRCA:The_Art_of_Game_Design_-_Jesse_Schell)): is an unanswered question visible, and is there one unannounced wonder every ten minutes **[I]**. Ueda's subtraction has a limit, and he said at a 2004 GDC Q&A that Ico may have gone too far **[S]** ([Glitterberri](https://glitterberri.com/ico/unused-dialogue-and-retranslation/)). Shadow of the Colossus had sixteen colossi and no side content; Wraith has six biomes and needs small rewards in the gaps **[I]**.

## Exploration and quests: terrain does the guiding

Breath of the Wild is the best-evidenced model for marker-free guidance, though the strongest details are secondhand. GMTK's reading of the GDC talk is that terrain is mostly cones and pyramids, so players choose to climb or go around, the eye goes to peaks where points of interest sit, and triangles occlude and then reveal **[S]** ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/how-nintendo-solved-zeldas-open-world)). The same source says the original ~15 towers made playtesters feel on a "guide rope," with heat maps showing ~80% following the route **[S, unconfirmed against the talk]**. Fujibayashi's "multiplication" versus "addition" contrast and Dohta's chemistry rules are better sourced **[P]** ([Game Watch Impress](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1047403.html)). Elden Ring complements this: Miyazaki set boss positions early and designed the map around boss gameplay **[P]** ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/elden-ring-director-reveals-his-favorite-boss-and-open-world-inspiration)), and Bycer's analysis credits fewer but richer points of interest in tiers, with no tower-unlock chore **[S, opinion]** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-elden-ring-succeeds-by-ignoring-20-years-of-open-world-design)). One unverified term is "gravity": a blogger reads it as key places sitting in basins so players drift down toward them, and the blogger admits exceptions **[S, interpretation]** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/breath-of-the-wild-open-world-analysis-gravity-to-go-forward)). Treat it as a hypothesis to test, not Nintendo doctrine.

For Wraith, the application is to sculpt each biome's heightfield so the next point of interest hides behind a rise, with a distinctive silhouette (ice spire, glass shard, vent) at each peak; give each biome one unmistakable global landmark and local landmarks visible only within a few hundred metres; use three tiers of site (single-reward nook, mini-site, Warden-scale set piece) instead of identical checklist icons; and place the Wardens first and build terrain around them, as Miyazaki did **[I]**. Guidance should be diegetic first. Ghost of Tsushima's Guiding Wind replaced the minimap, but an accessibility review notes wind streaks lose contrast in snow **[S]** ([Can I Play That?](https://caniplaythat.com/2020/07/29/ghost-of-tsushima-accessibility-review/)), which matters for a frost steppe, so Wraith needs per-biome high-contrast variants such as dark motes on snow **[I]**.

Progression should gate by knowledge and visible locks, not items. Outer Wilds ties progress to what the player learns and logs, and Obra Dinn confirms deductions only in groups of three to block brute force **[S]** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn)). Team Cherry's Ari Gibson says they loosened hard gates in Hollow Knight so many power-ups became optional **[S, search summary; verify before quoting]** ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-design-a-great-metroidvania-map)). Because your biome order is open, make every lock visible before its key (frozen door, lava seam, sealed glass vault), give shortcuts and a map-pin tool for return trips, and soft-gate with hazard and skill rather than walls **[I]**. A "Remembrance" journal that records observations, not tasks, makes knowledge the key **[I]**.

For side quests, the best-sourced guidance comes from Witcher 3. Sasko's GDC 2023 lessons are to withhold information so players want the story, favour emotional impact over spectacle, let the world carry exposition, and make consequences visible and delayed **[P via summary]** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/10-key-takeaways-from-the-quest-design-of-cyberpunk-2077-and-the-witcher-3)). The team also cut gameplay with no narrative purpose **[S]** ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/94509/witcher-3-quest-designers-talk-design-process-most-difficult-quest-to-create)). Smith and Worch's environmental storytelling talk frames it as players inferring "what happened here" from spaces that feel historical **[S]** ([Nieman Storyboard](https://niemanstoryboard.org/2011/01/14/harvey-smith-on-environmental-storytelling-and-embedding-narrative/)). A data-driven quest template follows: an anomaly hook, a staged tableau to investigate, one choice, and a delayed visible consequence such as a lit hearth or a new cairn **[I]**. Apply a no-fetch test (if the verb is only carry, add a reveal or choice) and reuse elemental verbs (thaw, ignite, calm) as quest verbs **[I]**. Prefer a few recurring characters, such as a pilgrim met three times across biomes, to many one-off givers, and avoid irreversible quest failure given the quiet tone **[I]**. Note that sourcing on Yakuza substories, Tsushima Tales and Elden Ring NPC questlines was not found, so none is relied on.

## Wardens: vary the problem, then the verb

The Shadow of the Colossus evidence is unusually strong. Each colossus started as a planning document covering body shape, climbable fur, battle flow and arena together, and Ueda said designs could not be judged until modelled and tested, with failures often forcing arena changes **[P, Famitsu republication]** ([Glitterberri](https://glitterberri.com/extended-developer-interview/making-of-the-colossi/)). The roster went 48, then 24, then 16, and Kaido describes condensing "the individual elements of two Colossi down into one" **[P]** (same source). Ueda wanted early colossi beatable without terrain and later ones to need it **[P]**, and rejected item gating because it would send players back to the field **[P]**. An analyst's breakdown gives the rhythm of search (horseback, no music), approach (quiet, oppressive), then fight (percussive), and notes only 2 of 16 do not require climbing to the weak point **[S]** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-art-of-shadow-of-the-colossus-2-6-gameplay-and-story)); that article's claim that no new power is ever introduced is internally disputed, so treat it cautiously. Monster Hunter adds that behaviour is planned first and that expression and emotional state signal intent **[P]** ([Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/news/2015/02/monster_hunter_4_ultimate_director_discusses_monster_design_and_animation)), and its monsters grow out of habitat and environment **[S]** ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2017/11/15/monster-hunter-world-bestiary-day-2-the-shocking-tobi-kadachi)).

From that, a varied-boss recipe is to change five axes per Warden: the discovery puzzle, the arena, the win condition, the tempo, and the approach phase **[I]**. A comparison table makes the Long Walker's position concrete.

| Axis | Frost mammoth (existing) | Bellwether (existing) | Long Walker (proposed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target state | Static | Static siege structure | Moving, six-legged, always in motion |
| Win condition shape | Freeze joints, shatter | Strip vents, climb neck, three valves | Shape the sand, stumble a leg, then climb |
| Discovery puzzle | Which joints | Which order of strip and valve | Reading the gait to find the leg that can be fooled |
| Arena | Frozen ground | Manor grounds | Open dune; the Walker's wake is the terrain |
| Tempo | Deliberate | Staged | Continuous pursuit, with calm at a footfall's end |
| Approach | Standard | Siege approach | Long quiet sand-surf with a distant silhouette |

The Long Walker design is all **[I]**. Its gait is the readable telegraph, following Monster Hunter's principle that movement signals intent, and each footfall shows a leg-lift before the hazard. The player surfs its wake and dune slopes, uses surface-by-element rules to alter where a foot lands (Tide compacts, Ember glazes, Gale raises dust), stumbles a leg by placing a ridge in its path (an analogue of Shadow of the Colossus's bow-to-the-soles second colossus), and climbs the leg only afterward. This keeps the Walker verb-only, like Shadow of the Colossus and unlike Zelda's dungeon-item-boss pattern **[S]** ([Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/aonuma-discusses-zelda-dungeon-design/)), using a skill the player already learned in the world. If ideas multiply, apply Kaido's merge-not-add rule and condense two into one. Checkpoint at phase boundaries (each stumbled leg), keep the uncheckpointed approach short, and scale camera shake and sound delay with Warden scale **[I]**. Shake follows Swink's definition of game feel as real-time control with polish and a roughly 100 ms responsiveness window **[S]** ([Wikipedia: Game feel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_feel)); frame counts for hit-stop, coyote time and input buffering are standard practice but unsourced here and need tuning in the build.

## Systems, combat and traversal share one rule table

The surface-by-element table is Wraith's chemistry engine. Dohta's Breath of the Wild rules are that elements change materials, elements change each other, and materials never change materials; players accept rules that are consistent and clever, and unified rules produce solutions developers never planned **[P via GDC recap]** ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-17-breath-of-the-wild-science-lies)). Noita (local pixel rules) **[S]** ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/noita-a-game-based-on-falling-sand-simulation/)) and Divinity: Original Sin 2 (surfaces that abilities combo with) **[S]** ([Co-Optimus](https://www.co-optimus.com/article/14154/pax-east-2016-divinity-original-sin-2-hands-on-impressions.html)) follow the same pattern. Adopt the three rules as invariants so the table stays auditable, route Warden and enemy solutions through it so they are discoverable not scripted, and teach each reaction once in a safe sandbox before an encounter requires it **[I]** (the sources give no teaching method).

For enemies, keep a small roster with distinct roles. Doom Eternal's team treats enemies as pieces that "push you in different ways," uses placement to force weapon switching, and makes enemies the resource source **[P]** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-aggressive-resource-management-of-i-doom-eternal-i-)); its "chess pieces" quote comes via secondary coverage **[S]** ([Fandom](https://www.fandom.com/articles/doom-eternal-marty-stratton-hugo-martin)). Sunset Overdrive had to redesign enemies for a highly mobile player **[S]** ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1021780/contactUs)). For Wraith with at most eight enemies, give each a role and one forced behaviour; make material the lever (a Stone Shaped needs Shatter or Gale erosion, a Frost Shaped is slowed) as the weapon-switch analogue; refill focus from reactions and traversal chains; and give surf-arena enemies lunges or ranged lines that cut a surf path, so mobile players face mobile threats. Vary the same pool by surface state (frozen, sand, burning turf) for freshness **[I]**.

Traversal needs its own core pleasure. Insomniac's Smith says Spider-Man players spend most of their time swinging, that release timing is a mechanic, that speed is conveyed through camera and parallax, that districts shape the swing's texture, and that one-button traversal would remove engagement **[P]** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/making-insomniac-s-i-spider-man-i-do-what-a-spider-can)). Titanfall 2's team tuned wall-running so speed accumulates when chained **[P]** ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2016/08/24/respawn-is-making-changes-to-titanfall-2-based-on-player-concerns)). For Wraith: different surf rules per biome (Frost fast and slick, Tide slow and packed), chaining surf into glide into updraft refilling focus, and sand-surf speed gated by slope and the element-written surface so terrain state feeds both traversal and combat **[I]**. Journey's sand is a reference for how surface behaviour carries emotion **[S]** ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/an-interview-with-journey-co-creator-jenova-chen-video/)).

## Claude skills: nothing official, two worth adapting

Anthropic's own skills repository has 19 skill folders and none about games **[P]** ([anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/tree/main/skills)). Among community packs, the most design-literate is saschb2b's `game-design` (MIT, but only 20 stars). It offers an eight-axis analysis lens, MDA, nested loops, flow, game feel, economy, around 24 patterns from 17 dissected games, critique "smells" (dominant strategy, no opportunity cost, hollow loop, treadmill), and a concept bundle requiring 3-5 pillars that each name what they reject, with every feature serving a pillar or being cut **[P]** ([repo](https://github.com/saschb2b/skills)). Donchitos' Claude-Code-Game-Studios (MIT, about 27k stars, 49 agents and 74 skills by its README) is heavier; its `game-designer` agent covers MDA, self-determination theory, flow, nested 30-second, 5-15 minute and session loops, and an 8-section design-doc standard, and its `/balance-check` returns "NOT ASSESSED" rather than a false pass when inputs are absent **[P]** ([game-designer.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Donchitos/Claude-Code-Game-Studios/main/.claude/agents/game-designer.md); [balance-check](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Donchitos/Claude-Code-Game-Studios/main/.claude/skills/balance-check/SKILL.md)). Its flaws are real: engine sets cover Godot, Unity and Unreal only (no Babylon or web), agents share one context, and its own README sets default rigor to minimal because heavier process did not produce a better game **[P/S]** ([repo](https://github.com/Donchitos/Claude-Code-Game-Studios); [Substack review](https://gameproductionalchemist.substack.com/p/claude-code-game-studios-49-agents)). The gamedev-skills marketplace has `level-design`, `game-feel`, `dialogue-systems` and an `rpg` skill that skews toward implementation (stats, inventory, quest state), judged from a listing only **[S]** ([claudeskills.info](https://claudeskills.info/skills/gamedev-skills/awesome-gamedev-agent-skills/rpg/)). Several other listings (jarrodmedrano `game-feel` and `level-design`, majiayu000 `game-design-theory`) were not read and are unverified. Nothing found specialises in open-world action RPG, boss or quest design.

The notes' recommendation, which I endorse, is not to install any pack wholesale, since the model already knows MDA and flow and the value is in checklists and critique prompts **[I]**. Read saschb2b and the CCGS designer and balance-check files, then write a short project skill `wraith-design` **[I]**. It should hold pillars that each name what they reject, a feature gate (serve a pillar, feed a loop layer, survive the smells list), the loop stack, a Warden brief covering the five variation axes, the surface-by-element table with its three invariants, a quest template with the no-fetch test, a game-feel and latency checklist tuned for 1440p/60, and a playtest procedure built on the existing `?bot=1` harness with a NOT ASSESSED rule. Anthropic's skill docs support keeping SKILL.md short with details in `references/` **[P]** ([Claude docs](https://www.claude.com/docs/skills/how-to)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which framework wins" to "which frameworks agree." They converge on a few testable commitments: verbs that are fun with no content around them, a rule table small enough to audit, variation spent on problems rather than tools, and quiet treated as a designed resource. The Long Walker is the first Warden where this is a decision, not a preference, because making it a moving target is what stops the roster from collapsing into "freeze it, strip it, climb it."

The largest open risks are evidentiary and tonal. Several load-bearing claims (the "gravity" concept, Korok density, the tower heat-map figures, Hollow Knight gating, Doom's "chess pieces" wording) rest on secondary summaries and should be re-checked before they drive decisions. Every mapping to Wraith, including the Long Walker, is untested inference, and Ueda's own admission that subtraction can go too far argues for playtesting the empty stretches with the bot and with humans, then tuning the mechanics (the only thing designers directly control under MDA) rather than adding content.
